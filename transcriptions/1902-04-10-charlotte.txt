Ladies and Gentlemen:

I am very glad [-[?af?]-] to have the chance of greeting you tonight to say how much I appreciate your having come.    (A voice: "How about the Mecklenburgh Declaration?" )   The President  :  "The Mecklenburgh Declaration was all right."   (Great Applause)   Then, after a pause--Some one in the audience has spoken of the Mecklenburgh Declaration.  That was the first declaration of independence in any of what are now the United States.    (Applause)   To my good fortune I have studied much of the early history of North Carolina.   I know the part you played in the Revolution;  the part you played even during the Revolution in winning the great West for the republic.   I feel that you deserve to be called in a peculiar sense Americans of Americans, and no higher praise than that can be given in our country.  I have appreciated greatly the greetings I have received , not merely in South Carolina, but from the representatives of your state who were there also.    (Applause)

It has been to me a privilege to meet you.  Yesterday I reviewed your national guard at the Charleston Exposition.   It was a pleasure to be able to see them.   I have served with some of your men, for some men born in this state were in my regiment.  On one occasion I had to choose out 20 sharp shooters;  two of them were North Carolinans.    (Great applause)

Here the train started, and the President concluding,said: " I thank you and wish you all good luck."  (Great applause)
