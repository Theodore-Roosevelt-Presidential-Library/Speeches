Senator Ankeny; and you, My Friends and Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to meet you and to be coming through this wonderful valley into your city, [-[illegible]-] and to see what you are doing with sheep, cattle, wheat, alfalfa, sugar, fruit. It is perfectly astounding; and I cannot sufficiently congratulate you and all our people upon the way in which you have been able literally to make the wilderness blossom like the rose. Fifty years ago, Senator, when you first came here with cattle it would not have seemed possible that a community like this would have grown up; it would not have seemed possible to develop gre at wheat fields and now to develop sugar, [-that-][+which+] is going to be a tremendous industry. It would not have seemed possible to develop what you have been able to by the use of water on this soil. You have had the physical advantages; you have had the water and the soil; but above [+all+] you have had the man to take advantage of them. That is what counts in the long run-- to have the man with common sense and with tenacity of purpose to make him able to use what there is ready to hand. The best gifts that[-xxxxx-] nature can give are wasted if the man has not the quality that makes him take advantage of them. That is what is needed most.

I {wantto|want to} say one other thing, {nand|and} that is what a pleasure it has given me to see here in these communities where I am greeted always by the men of the Grand Army, by the men of the great past, how you are taking thought for the future in the way you are providing for your children, [-that you are-] providing for the next generation. The future of this country of course depends fundamentally upon the type of citizenship you get out of the next generation. If the boys and girls of to-day turn out the right kind of men and women of to-morrow we are going to go up as a nation. (Applause)
