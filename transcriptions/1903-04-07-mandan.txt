My Fellow-Citizens:

I cannot say what pleasure it is to see you, to be back among you again. As I have traveled west through North Dakota to-day I have felt more and more that it was getting to be a good deal like coming back home.(Applause.) And from now on there will be few places where I shall stop in which I will not have some old friends among the people that greet me. I have been in your city in the old days. I {c ame|came} to North Dakota first twenty-three years ago; and for a number of years I live[+d+] out to the west here,on the Little Missouri, so I know your state pretty well. I know its people and I am proud of you. I have watched with keenest interest your growth and prosperity, and how you have gone. I am glad to see the success you have made of whatever you have turned your hands to- fine wheat growing and cattle raising and the like. But of all your crops I like the children best. (Cheers and {appl ause|applause}.) They seem to be all right in quality and in quantity, and that is what counts-the kind of men and women you are going to {h ave|have} in this state. That is the thing that counts, and as I like the stock I am glad to see it kept up.

I want to congratulate you upon the work that has been done in your schools. I have been struck by the public schools, the high schools, the normal schools all as I have come along to-day. The growth has been wonderful and it has been characteristic of the state-I may say of our people-that hand in hand with the material development has gone the upbuilding of the social and intellectual life,without which the material development can count for so little. We have a right to demand in a state and in a nation wisdom from the law makers; good legislation; honest and upright administration. Something can be done by law. If the laws are bad or are administered corruptly or {inefficientky|inefficiently} or timidly, it will be bad for all of us.

But when is all said and done, the chief factor in any man's success must be that man's own individual qualities, his individual character. You men and women of North Dakota have built up this

[pb 95 425:0273]

state because you have had the stuff in you to do it; because you have not been discouraged by adversity and hardship; because you have had the spirit that dares , the spirit that does; you have had endurance and physical hardihood and [-xx-] resolution, strength of mind, strength of body, strength of soul. If it had not been for those {qualitiesyou|qualities you} could not have won out in the contest with nature; you could not have made this state what it is. It has been a hard struggle-in many ways the work of the frontiersman always is hard; but then,gentlemen, I think that you will find that in the long run whatever is worth doing is hard. If it is an easy job it is not usually worth having. {Th e|The} men that win out are the men who are able and willing to work.

I am very glad to see you, men, women and children. I am proud of you and I wish you well. (Cheers and applause.)
