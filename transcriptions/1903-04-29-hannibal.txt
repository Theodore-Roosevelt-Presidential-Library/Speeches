Mr. Mayor; and you, My Fellow Americans; My Fellow Citizens:

{Itis|It is} indeed a pleasure to be here to-day, to have entered your mighty and beautiful state, and to make my first stop in this city. Like everyone else I feel as though I had an intimate acquaintance with this city as it was half a century ago, for I am a good reader of Mark Twain. I rejoice in coming here to be a witness of your growth, a witness of the prosperity which you have so abundantly enjoyed with the rest of our common country. And in thanking you , let me say a word of special thanks to the men of the National Guard who have turned out as a escort. I am glad {tosee|to see} you, and I am glad to see you in service uniform. I always like to see a guardsman when he looks like business; and among the things recently done in Congress I have felt like specially congratulating us upon at last having a militia law which will do much for us, [-[?and?]-] and among other things enable us to furnish the National Guard with proper arms. I do not want to see any American soldier with a black powder rifle, just exactly as I do not want to see him with a crossbow. I believe that any man who wears or [-xxx-] may in time of emergency wear Uncle Sam's uniform is entitled to the best that we can give him; (Applause) and having given him the arms I have got confidence enough in our people and in the soldier-like spirit of the officers and enlisted men of the national guard to be sure that by training and exercise he will see that he fits himself to get the best use out of it. As an old- {natonal|national} guardsman myself let me say that I hope you will keep steadily in mind in time of peace to prepare yourselves efficiently should trouble arise. I want to see the marksmanship of the guard get steadily above even its present standard. In a battle the shots that count are the shots that hit; and the man who is worth his weight is the man who is grown to handle his rifle so that it natural for him to handle it well. I want to see the officer and the enlisted man shoot. In battle the officer is not going to carry his sword in case he is being put against a serious foe . I {wantto|want to} see the {office|officer} and the enlisted man both shoot well, and I want to see the officer impressed with the fact that the shooting of the enlisted man , if he does it well, reflects credit upon him; that he must supervise it and be responsible for it. As I say, I have been in the national guard, I have served in it, and therefore I take a peculiar interest in all things pertaining thereto.

[pb 176 425:0313]

I want to congratulate you, my fellow citizens, men and women of Missouri, upon the way your city has grown. Missouri is fortunate in being a state of great and diversified resources, {gret|great} in agriculture, with a good deal of manufacturing, a great deal of commerce, and, here I am in one of the typical railway centers of the country. I would like to say a special word partly to and partly in connection with the railway men; the men who handle the [-xxxxx-] traffic of the country, who carry on the transportation business of the country show and need qualities which it is particularly desirable for us in our highly civilized life to see that our people retain. The man who is a good railroad man has got to be willing to take risks, to suffer exposure, to know what it is to face irregular hours, and finally,to combine the ability to obey orders and obey them quick with the ability to act on his own initiative, his own responsibility, {intime|in time} of need. At the present day when life is so easy for many {os uf|of us}, when there tends to be , owing to the growth of civilization, of comfort, and indeed of luxury, a certain softening of the fibers, it is a good thing to have certain professions in which the utmost possible demand is made upon all that is virile and manly in those that follow them. I believe emphatically in honesty, decency, cleanliness, morality as a basis for life; but it is not enough. I do not care how virtuous a man is, if he is afraid, he is no good. The {clpistered|cloistered} virtue does not count in this world. The man who is a nice fellow and never wrongs anybody and {sists|sits} at home in his own parlor and says how sorry he is the world is so bad, [-xxxx-] is of scant use to the body politic. What we need is the man who is a square man, who deals rightly with his neighbors, who deals rightly with the state, who is a good husband, a good father, a good man to deal with, a [-x-]good man to work with, and who in addition to being a good man, is a man plain and simple. (Applause) In other words, besides decency, honesty, morality, all of which are indispensable, we need the rough qualities , the quality of courage, the quality of strength, the quality of hardihood. You need decency, and that is not enough. You {needcourage|need courage}, and that is not enough. For I do not care how decent and how brave a man is if he is a natural born fool you can do nothing with him. You need decency, courage, and finally, the saving grace of common sense. Missouri has gone on and will go on because its men and women {possessall|possess all} three qualities. (Cheers and applause)
