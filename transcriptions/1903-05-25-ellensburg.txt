Mr. Mayor; and My Fellow Citizens; My Fellow Americans:

It is the greatest pleasure to be with you to-day. First of all let me greet those whom I know the others will not [-xxxxx-] grudge my specially greeting -- the men of the Grand Army and the representatives of those who did even more than the men of the civil war, the women; (Cheers and applause) for while the men went to battle[+,+] to the wom[-e-][+a+]n fell the harder task of seeing husband or {leer|lover}, father or brother, going to the war, and she herself hav[-e-][+ing+] to stay behind with the load of doubt, anxiety and uncertainty and often the hard difficulty of making both ends meet in the household while the breadwinner was away.

In a state like Washington with its record of active and vigorous life, with in it representatives of the men who fought as the men of the old war fought I do not wonder that her sons did so well in the Philippines and I am not surprised that you here should have sent a company [-[?in the regiment?]-] which as I understand it lost more than almost any other company of all the troops in the Philippines. They were my breth[-[illegible]-][+re+]n , my comrades in our war, which was a very little war compared to your[+s,+] but still we had a job to do and we did it. (Applause)

Let me say a special word of greeting to those at the other end -- the children. I have greeted the veterans, now I want to greet the children specially. I am very, very glad to see you. I have got just one word to say to you and it applies almost as well to your elders. I believe in play and I believe in work. Play hard while you play, and when you work do not play at all. (Applause) In congratulating y ou of Washington upon your lumber, your commerce, upon your great coal fields, upon the cattle on [-xxx-] [+a+] thousand hills, [-xx-] in congratulating you upon all your products I congratulate you most upon the children. They seem all right in quality and in quantity, and as I believe in your stock I am glad it is being kept up. (Applause) [-[?I am pleased?]-] It has pl eased me particularly coming through this mighty state with its extraordinary capacity for industrial development to see that[-xxx-] you, the men and women who are developing it, at the same time that you are taking the fullest advantage of your material resources are taking care to build upon those as a foundation the higher life, that you are taking thought for the next generati on, taking thought for the country, for the people that are to come after you. No men do their duty or pretend to do their duty if they simply think of their own interests, if they do not shape the governmental
[pb 91 425:0471] policy, their social policy, for the country as a whole, the country that is to come after them. Exactly as every man [-[illegible]-] worth his salt, or woman worth her salt, will care even more for the fu[-[illegible]-]ture of their children than for their own well-being, so in this nation we are bound in honor [-the-][+to+] shape our present policy with a view to the nation's future needs; to do as you did in the civil war. [-y-][+Y+]ou went to war, you faced four years of conflict, that the generations that came after you for the centuries to come should live in union and in peace within our borders. (Applause) So when you provide for the training and upbuilding of the children, when you provide for the schools -- the high school, the normal school, the academy, the college -- you are [-xxxx-] building firm and wide and deep the foundation for the might and greatness of the future; for after all[+,+] important though the material resources are, important though your wheat, your lumber, your fisheries, your cattle, your mines, your commerce, your fac[-[illegible]-][+to+]ries are, most important of all is the standard of good citizenship which you produce. I congratulate Washington upon its school system, upon all its schools from the simplest to the highest; and I say a word of special greeting to those engaged in teaching, to the teachers, the educators. No other body of men or women in time of peace does a more important work than those upon whose teaching and example and sympathy and self-abnegation and enthusiasm so much depends for the future generation. Yet the men and women must remember that you cannot put off upon the {teadhers|teachers} the whole duty of educating the next generation. Fathers and mothers must educate the children in their own homes by precept and by example, and just let me say this, that if your precepts and your examples [-[illegible]-][+differ+] you cannot expect good results for the children. There is no use in preaching truth where mendacity is sometimes practiced. There is no use preaching {unselfinhness|unselfishness} if the father consistently leads a selfish life. There is no use preaching the gospel of work if the father and mother shirk work. There is no use preaching the gospel of duty if there is no [-xxxx-] attempt to perform duty on the part of those who preach it. A father and mother have a duty in educating their children of which no one can relieve them. {Theteachers|The teachers} can do much, but after all it is the help in the [-xxx-] home which can do most. (Applause)

I also want to say a word upon the {kindsof|kinds of} quality {whoch|which} we need in citizenship[+,+] for [?we?] need more than one kind. In the first place yo u need decency, honesty, the spirit of fair dealing, the spirit {tht|that} makes a man a good neighbor, a good
[pb 92 425:0471] friend, the spirit that makes a man do his duty by the state. If you have not the foundation of clean living and fair dealing in you then the greater a man's ability is the worse he is for the whole community. It is just as it was in the civi l war; if the man had not the spirit of loyalty, of obedience, of faith to the flag, of faith to the nation, then the stronger , the abler, the more cunning he was, the more dangerous he was. In his regiment he was a cause of disturbance; outside the regiment he would come very near being a traitor to the nation. So in civic life, exactly as bodily strength if unaccompanied by any spirit of [-[illegible]-] self-restraint[-xxxx x xxx-] makes a man a dange rous brute, while at the same if guided aright it makes him a most valuable citizen, so mere intellectual ability, the power that makes a man able to rise in the world, his smartness, his business capacity, his shrewdness, if there is not a backing of moral sense behind it[+,+] makes him the more da gerous. In this count ry we urgently need to have it established that the weight of public {opinionshall|opinion shall} be felt just as heavily against the scoundrel who succeeds as against the scoundrel who fails. (Applause) But that is not enough. Exactly as in the civil war you needed patriotism first, but the patriotism did not count if the man ran away, so it is in civil life. In additi on to the spirit of decency , of fair dealing, of honesty, you must have the strong, virile virtues, the virtues that make a man able to hold his own in the world[-[?, to make your felt?]-], to make [-your-][+his+] weight felt as of moment in the larger life.of the nation. It is not enough to mean well when you sit at home. You have got to be able to do well when you get out into the actual field where civic life is waged; you have got to be able to do well in your trade, in business, to keep your family, to make yourself felt, to [-xxxxx-] [+bring up+] your children so that they shall go upward a little, to make yourself count whatever your part. The virtue that sits at home in its own parlor [-xxx-] and bemoans how bad the world is never yet benefitted anyone. What we need is the type of decent man who can go out and hold his own against all comers, who without losing his sense of decency can make himself felt as a man of weight wherever he is put. That applies in the education of children. Let me come back to that for just one moment. One thing that always [+makes+] me feel a little melancholy is [-[illegible]-][+to+] see fathers and mothers who themselves have worked hard and {leduseful|led useful} lives but who in a queer misguided spirit [-[illegible]-] of foolish affection, try to save their children from trouble by also {sving|saving} them from being of any earthly use in the world.
