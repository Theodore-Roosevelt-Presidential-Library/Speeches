My friends and neighbors:

I am more pleased than I can say to come back and have a chance to see you. The farther west I have gotten to-day, the more I have felt as if I was coming home. {(ZApplause)|(Applause)} It is just seventeen years ago that I came over here as orator of the day at the Fourth of July celebration. I remember it just as well as can be.

Now I want to say one word, especially, Captain to you and the members of the guard who have come down to greet me. Naturally, I have a peculiar feeling toward you. We have been comrades in effect together, (Applause)

There are a great many of you here whom I knew in the old days when I {usedto|used to} bring my cattle in to ship them from here. {Andin|And in} those days I do not think there was a puncher in the country that had not worked for me.

I do not know if you recollect once when I was a deputy sheriff and I brought in three {mn|men} ( A voice "I do")-- somebody says that he was one of {thembut|them, but} I am sure that is not so--and naturally I come back {hereand|here and} I feel that I am among my [-xxx-] old friends, the people whom I knew so well. All I regret is that I cannot spend a couple of hours shaking hands and talking over the past with you.

Knowing North Dakota so well ,I have watched all that she has done in the growth of the state with peculiar interest. I am proud of your success. I am proud of the way you have earned it. The changes since I first came to Dickinson [-xxx xxxxyxgxxxx-], which was twenty years ago, have been great. When I first came to Bismarck, the railroad did not come this far. Twenty years ago I first struck Medora; and one of your citizens repaired my rifle, with which I killed my first buffalo.
