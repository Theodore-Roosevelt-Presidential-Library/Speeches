Senator Allison; and Gentlemen;

[+I can say, and not merely in a diplomatic spirit, that+] I have thoroughly enjoyed this evening. It has been a great pleasure to visit your beautiful city.

I would like now to bear testimony to the way in which I have found the representatives of Iowa at the national capital approach questions of public interest. I have been able to ask both your Senators and Representatives for their aid in dealing with matters of moment to the nation. As a [-x-] whole I have found them interested in questions of public [-xxxxxx-][+policy+]; and I have found that they could guide, and counsel, and aid me in the matters that I deemed of moment to the nation at large, and that they were not merely bound by fourth class post offices. How I have felt toward your people I think it hardly nee[+d+]ful for me to say. When I came to choose that member of my Cabinet who occupies the position which taken year in and year out is of most vital consequence to the [-xxxxxx-] welfare of the nation, I felt that although I should not ordinarily care to take two men from the same state, yet that Mr. Shaw's qualifications for the position were so preeminent that in justice to the nation I could not pass him by. (Cheers and applause)

As for Secretary Wilson, (Cheers and applause) after having been with him for twenty-one months, and after having been intimately associated with various different national administrations and knowing many members of the Cabinet, I wish to state without reserve that in point of loyalty, of that pro[-[?v?]-][+b+]ity which makes one feel that one is better for being associated with him, that in point of sound common sense, not always a common quality, and [-x-] in capacity to handle his office in the way that one would wish all offices of the government to be handled, if one could have ideal conditions, I have never met any man in public life who I feel quite came up to Secretary Wilson. (Cheers and applause) I cannot overstate the degree [-[illegible]-][+[?to?]+] which I lean on him and the personal affection that I feel for him. (Applause)

In thanking {youfor|you for} the reception accorded me here in your city, let me also say how much I have enjoyed the dinner.
