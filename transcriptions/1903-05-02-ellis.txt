Mr. Mayor; Senator Long; Ladies and Gentlemen:

I have enjoyed to the full passing through Kansas to-day and yesterday afternoon, and as I go through I am struck more and more by the great possibilities of your beautiful state. I have now come into the short-grass counties . I know the region well for in the old days I was in the cattle business myself. Then we had [-x-][+a+] very different kind of animals on the range[-[illegible]-]s from those I have seen from the car windo[-[illegible]-][+w+]s as I passed. In the old days we saw nothing approaching to the groves of trees and the great alfalfa fields that I have also watched. Above all, in congratulating you upon your material well-being, I wish[-x-] to congratulate you upon the type of citizenship here. (Applause) It is, after all, what we had a right to expect from a state within whose limits[+there+] are to be found so many representatives of the Grand Army of the Republic, of the men who proved their loyalty when the nation's life was at stake. The work of the pioneer is in its essentials the same as the work of the soldier. The same qualities of hardihood, of daring, of endurance, of iron resolution, were needed to [?turn?] the prairie into this great state as were needed in the civil war. One lesson that [-xxx-] you men of the Grand {Armytaught|Army taught} we in turn must learn . I speak in a [-[?g?]-][+t+]own of wageworkers, a great railway center, surrounded by a pastoral and agricultural population, a town that symbolizes and represents the complex industrial life of our country.

You won in the civil war because you had not only the stern, rugged qualities, but you had also the spirit of brotherhood, the spirit of each paying heed to his brother's well-being as well as his own. We stand at the top pitch of industrial life. We can keep it unless we mar it by our own folly, and the surest way in which to mar it is by paying heed to the counsels of envy, of jealousy, of arrogance, towards those who are less well off than ourselves, or of rancor towards those who are better off. Townsman and farmer, employer and employed, we must make up our minds that we shall rise or fall together. In the civil war you won because you fought for a common object and fought with all your heart. There have been wars and plenty of them in the world's history where one side has been beaten because its soldiers or generals were more jealous of one another than they were hostile to the enemy. When a general gets to the point of being more anxious to see a rival general humiliated than to see the

[pb 46 425:0348]

enemy defeated his usefulness is gone. If one wing of the army was beaten, the whole army fell back in disorder. So it {isin|is in} our industrial life. If disaster comes to one important section of the community it will come to all. If a period of bad times and depression comes all of us will feel it alike, and I earnestly counsel good sense, moderation, the effort on the part of each to look not only to his own interest but to the interest of others, to all parties in our great industrial centers, to all people engaged in doing their share of the industrial work of the day. It is not possible for me to be more specific, because in each locality the needs vary, the conditions vary, and in every locality there is urgent need that we should face these varying conditions with certain principles firmly established in our hearts,[+--+] the principle of justice, the principle of equity, the principle of fair and square dealing as between man and man, the desire each to do as well as he can for himself, but each to make no demand which it is impossible for the other to grant and the making of which and the refusal of which may mean in the long run the widest disaster to all.

There is a tendency to believe that law can do everything. It cannot. Good laws can do something. {Godd|Good} administration of the laws can do something more. We can of course always keep this as it must be kept-- a country of liberty under the law, liberty through and by the law. Law can prevent injustice by one man against another. Law can secure a fair deal so far as human wisdom can secure it, a fair chance for each man to show under reasonably favorable conditions the quality that there is in him. More than that the law cannot do. In the last analysis a man's success must depend primarily now as it has depended since the world began, upon that man's own individual qualities, upon his courage, his hardihood, his thrift, his resolution, his power to work. If he has not got these qualities in him, no law can put them in him. If the man is a shiftless farmer, if he is a [-xxxx-][+slack+] and indifferent worker, if he is a foolish or extravagant business man , no law can help him. No law that [+[?was?]+] ever devised will make a coward brave or a fool wise or a weakling strong. All that the law can do is while preventing injustice, to give each man the chance to show whether he is a fool or a wise man, a coward or a brave man, a strong man or a weakling. If you have not got the right stuff in you, then nothing in the world can get it out of you, because it is not there to get it out. Success has come here

[pb 47 425:0348]

to those who worked hard and [?knew?] how to work, not to the man who has always bemoaned that fate was so bad to him. You know fate is not kind if you sit down and weep at the fact that things have not been better. They grow worse when fate is bad. Grin and bear it, take it that way, and strive to make it better. While a sound body is a good thing, and a sound mind an even better thing, the best of all things is character,[+--+] [-c-][+C+]haracter[+,+] by which we mean the sum of the qualities that test a man's worth as a citizen. I do not care how intelligent a man is, if at the first failure he lies down you cannot do much with him. The man that wins is the man that {nas|has} in him the energy and resolution that make him willing and resolute to try again, and try again, until success comes . To all of us disaster comes at times. There is not a man here {whodoes|who does} not at times slip or stumble, and shame to his fellow who will not stretch out his hand. Each of us at times needs a helping hand, and each of us should then be ready to stretch out that helping hand. Help the man up, get him on the right path, but if he lies down you cannot carry him. You can help him in the long run efficiently in but one way-- you can help him to help himself. That is the kind of help that in the long run counts for most. Character includes [-xx-] many qualities[+,+] but three most of all.[+--+] In the first place, decency, honesty, not merely the honesty that does not steal, but the honesty that makes a man a good father, a good husband, a good neighbor, a good man to deal with, or a good man to work with, or a good man to work along side of, the quality that makes a man scorn aught that is not fair and honorable in dealing with his neighbors. And that is not enough. In war you needed to have a man patriotic first, you needed to have him have love of country; but it did not make any difference how much he loved his country if he ran away he was no good. I do not care how virtuous a man is, how decent he is, if he is afraid, you can do little with him. We need to have virtue, honesty, morality, decency, and with[+it+] we need strength, courage, fearlessness, the iron resolution that drives a man forward to success . In addition to honesty we need courage in its widest sense, and we need something in addition to that, for it makes but little difference if a man be both honest [-xx-] and [-[illegible]-][+brave,+] if he is a fool you can do but {littl e|little} with him. You need honesty, you need courage, and finally you need the saving grace of common sense.

I believe in you, I believe in your future, because I believe you have just

[pb 48 425:0349]

those qualities, and I think you have the sturdiness of fiber which enables each of you to try to work out his own fate successfully, and yet that lift toward generous and righteous living which makes each of you anxious to deal fairly with his neighbor. I believe in {yor|you} because I think that you are individually fine and good citizens; and because such is my belief, I feel that you will win in the future an even greater share of moral and material well-being than that which we now so {ab ndantly|abundantly} enjoy. I wish you well, I believe in you, and I thank you for having come here to-day. ({Cherrs|Cheers} and applause)
