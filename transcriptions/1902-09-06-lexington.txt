My friends and fellow citizens:    I am glad to have the chance of seeing you even only for a minute or two this evening.   I wish it had been possible for me to time my trip through Kentucky so as to have come here by daylight, but it was not.   In {Kantucky|Kentucky} any American cannot but feel a peculiar sense of kinship with the past, for Kentucky has contributed far more than the share we would expect from its population to the leadership of the country in peace and in war.   This city commemorates by its name the spot where the first victory was won in the war that made this country a nation, and it is eminently fitting that the victory of Lexington in Massachusetts should be commemorated here in Kentucky - Kentucky[+,+] which stands in the heart of the country, which belongs in a peculiar sense alike to the north and [-xx-] the south, the east and the west - Kentucky[+,+] whose pioneers won this state for our country in the stress of the Revolutionary War itself.   [-Here in Kentucky-] you have practiced the qualities not merely that make good soldiers but that make good citizens.   There is no one quality that makes for good citizenship by itself.   In the first place, you must have honesty, decency.   If a man is not straight, then the abler he is the more dangerous he is to the people as a whole; and our country will be put on the plane where we desire to see her only when once for all it is understoo[-[?s?]-][+d+] that we visit an even heavier odium upon the scoundrel who succeeds than upon the scoundrel who fails.   Honesty by itself is not enough.   In addition to honesty you need other qualities.   I don't care how good a man is, if he is afraid he is of no use (Cheers and applause). In addition to honesty you must have courage - moral as well as physical - the courage that dares and the courage that endures - not the courage that flashes like a fire of straw[+, but+] the courage that remains steadfast in purpose to the end - the courage that has in it the iron fiber of resolution. Courage and honesty - no man is worth his salt as a citizen unless he has both.   And both of them together aren't enough.   I don't care how brave and how honest a man is, if he is a natural born fool you can do little with him (Laughter and applause).   You must have courage and honesty, and to

[pb 19 424:0691]

them both you must add the saving grace of common sense.

[-[illegible]-] I can say that to you without feeling that I am preaching to you, because I feel that I am merely giving expression to the beliefs that you and I hold in common.   Good laws are good things, but more important still is the good citizenship that must stand behind the law.   It is the same in civic life that it is in military life.   The men here who fought in the civil war, whether they wore the blue or whether they wore the gray, carried weapons different from the weapons carried by the men who followed Washington.   They were drilled with different {tacticw|tactics}. They wore different uniforms.   But the spirit that drove them to battle was the same.   The men who won undying honor because of their courage and self-devotion in the civil war were spurred onward by the same lofty impulses that won victory for Washington's troops at Yorktown.   And so now, as Colonel Williams will tell you, if we ever have, which I not only hope but believe we will not have, war with a foreign power, our troops would have to be armed with the small caliber high-power modern rifle, and instead of the old shoulder to shoulder drill they would be drilled in open order; but if they are to win, no matter what their drill and their weapons, they will win because they have in them the stuff of the men who fought from '61 to '65 (Applause).   I hate to see, as one generally does see, the national guards of most of our states armed with the black powder musket. You might almost as well have crossbows.   But if you take a man and give him instead of the black powder musket the best modern rifle, and if the man himself hasn't got the right stuff in him, a good man will beat him with a club (Laughter).   Do not forget that.   Of course if two good men meet[+,+] the man with the good rifle will win; but the rifle is not a substitute for the qualities that make the soldier.   The rifle simply rounds them out. It is just the same way with our citizenship.   It is a mighty good and necessary thing to have good laws.   It is a mighty good thing [-xx-][+to+] have the fundamental law which we call the constitution.   There are other countries whose constitutions and laws are modelled on ours, [-who-] [+which+] have yet made pretty [pb 20 424:0692] nearly a failure of free government, because they do not have the same type of citizenship as ours.   Back of the law, back of the constitution, you must have a high average of individual citizenship, for the lack of which nothing can atone.   And remember, as I have said before, that [-with-][+[?in?]+] that citizenship, though intellect counts somewhat, character counts more; and in character the three chief ingredients are honesty, courage, and common sense (Applause).
