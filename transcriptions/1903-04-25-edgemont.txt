Mr. [?Gossett?], and you, my fellow-citizens.

I am indeed glad to be greeted by you this {afternoo n|afternoon}, and to have the chance of saying a few words to you. I stand in that [-[illegible]-] corner of South Dakota which makes it the third gold producing state in the Union. I address not only pioneers, but th[+e+] successors and children of pioneers-the men who as miners and stock growers and cattle raisers and as farmers have opened this continent to civilization. A peculiar [-mede-][+meed+] of [-xxxxx-] praise must ever attach to the first man who does a difficult deed; the man who shows the way to those who come after how to conquer, whether the conquest be over man or nature.

To-day I was greeted by {ahd|and} given an escort by the old soldiers, some from the sanitarium at the Hot {S pri gs|Springs}; others who wear the button that shows that they fought in the army, but who now come here as among the pioneers from the Black Hills. Those men did what was in its essentials the same type of work that was done by the men who on foot or on horseback,{ddriving|driving} the great canvas-topped wagons, or [-[?polling?]-][+poleing+] [-xx-] and paddling on the rivers did the pioneer work of this country. I do not have to say in addressing men who fought in the great war that while honor attaches to all the victorious {amy|army}, yet that a peculiar [-mede-][+meed+] of honor attaches to the regiment, the brigade,the division that first smashes through the enemy's line and makes the victory possible. (Cheers and applause.) It is just so with the pioneers. Honor to all good citizens, but honor most of all to the men who took the lead in [-[illegible]-] taming the shaggy wilderness, to the trail blazers, to the men who first in the woods marked out that earliest of highways, the spotted line, the blazed trail, the men who first on horseback steered across the great lonely plains and drove their cattle up to feed upon the grounds from which the buffalo had not yet vanished. Some of you men on {hors back|horseback} there,possibly,certainly some,of the men whom I see before me in the audience were {enaged|engaged} in the cow business when I first came to the Little Missouri; and I [pb 115 425:0283] remember well in 1883 when a friend of mine {new|now} living in Kansas City,Major Towers, took a great band of trail cattle up on the Little Missouri, while [-haxxxx-] riding at the head of them he shot one of the last buffalo seen in that section. The cattle literally tred on the [-[illegible]-] heels of the buffalo. The cow punchers who took them in were {mking|making} ready the way for the civilization that was to come after them: To you from the Black Hills, who coming here in 1876 or at the time immediately around that date, [-[illegible]-] have developed the wonderful mines of that region, [-[illegible]-] have built up in what was then an Indian haunted wilderness cities like Deadwood and Lead,[-[illegible]-] it has fallen to your lot to see one of the great epoch feats of the century. You are among that body of men who conquered a continent for a race,and a people who [-[illegible]-] did your full share in making the nineteenth century the most wonderful upon which the sun has ever shone. The pioneer days have gone, but the need of the old pioneer virtues remains the same as ever.(Applause. ) You won, and you could only win because you had in you the stuff out of which strong men are made. In the days of 1876 here in the Black Hills the room for the weakling or the coward was small, and now the man who will do decent work here must be the man that has got the stuff in him. And I will say this much, I do not care what kind of law we have, or what kind of education, if the man has not got the stuff in him you cannot get it out of him, because it is not there.(Applause.)

We need good laws; we need education, with every bit of outside help that we can get, but fundamentally we have to rely each of us upon the qualities that go to make up the individual man. Something can be done by law. Let me take an illustration {fight|right} here from the country immediately around Edgemont. Passing in the last half hour through the valley of the Cheyenne I was impressed with the marvelous possibilities of agriculture which that valley has. We are not in the arid region. We are in the semi-arid region where there is plenty of water,only it is not equally distributed in point of time. Now it is in a country just like this that we can expect to see the benefit ultimately of that wise measure passed by the last Congress-the Irrigation act. (Cheers and applause.) [pb 116 425:0283]

There is plenty of water in the Cheyenne but it does not stay there. A proper system of storage reservoirs and a proper irrigation system, by {whihh|which} the water can be stored and distributed at the proper time will turn,in the lifetime even of those of us who are middleaged ,that valley of the Cheyenne into a singularly fruitful farming valley. [-[illegible]-] (Applause.)

I am speaking to you pioneers of the Black Hills, to you, the men of the mines, the men who have upbuilded the cities there, the men who have developed largely by irrigation the agriculture round the Black Hills; I speak to the pastoral and agricultural people here at Edgemont, so that this audience represents typically the entire western people; the people not only of South Dakota, but of the states in the same tier and of the states to the west, the states of the plains and of the Rocky Mountains. I know you well. I believe in you with all my heart.(Applause.) In my own time I have been in the round up with gentlemen like those over there(pointing to the cow punchers); and I understand that we are to have a typical exhibition a little later to show that the modern cow puncher is just as good as his predecessor was[+;+] and indeed I have been promised a little lunch at the round-up wagon. It has been my good fortune to work with you, to live with you, to see much of you. I admire the products of this great western country, but what I admire it most for is its men and its women, and as I believe in the stock I am mighty glad to see the children.

In closing I shall merely again say how glad I am to see you and to receive your welcome, and I wish you good luck with all my heart. (Cheers and applause.)
