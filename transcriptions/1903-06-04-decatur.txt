My Fellow Citizens:

I count myself fortunate in being given the privilege of dedicating this university. We are all as Americans to be congratulated that there are among us men who so worthily apply the fortunes that they have made[+,+] in devoting a portion of their fortunes to the cause of education in this country.

Mr. Millikin, I feel that as an American it is proper for me to express to you and to those like you the obligation that good Americans feel for what you and they have done in this university and in other educational institutions throughout the land. (Applause) I am especially pleased that I am to take part in [-xxxx-] the dedication of an institution of learning where so much teaching is to be with a direct view to the industrial betterment of the community. Ours is an age of specialization; and the man who is to do the highest industrial work will find himself {imme asurably|immeasurably} better prepared for it, if he can have [-[?had?]-] the proper kind of industrial training. I congratulate all of the men and women whom I address because this building consecrated to such a purpose is being erected here; for not only does such an institution benefit the student, benefit those supposed to be the only [-be-]beneficiaries, but it also indirectly helps all of us; because it becomes at once a center for the diffusion [-[?of learning, the diffusion?]-] of education.

Now I wish to say a word to the students, to those who will profit by what has been done. To whom much has been given, from him much shall rightly be expected. The man who has received an education which he owes in part either to the state or to private gift, has accepted a favor which {aw|as} a self-respecting American citizen he is bound to return; and he can return it in but one way. There is no return he can make to his alma mater of a tangible kind. There is no return he can make in the way of physical repayment of what has been spent upon his education either to the state or to the [-xxxxx-] individual donor, to the benefactor of the university. The return must be made in the shape of good citizenship in the community. The return for the education must take the form [?of?] the highest possible achievement on the part of the man who has received the [?education?].
