[-Gentlemen and ladies-]

Mr. Mayor, and My Fellow Citizens:

I am glad to have the chance of meeting an audience like this, and saying a few words to you. I have thoroughly enjoyed coming through your great and beautiful state. I enjoy seeing all of you and I know the rest of you will not grudge my saying a special word[-x-] of thanks to the men of the grand Army of the Republic for coming out to greet me, (Cheers and applause) for they showed in war the qualities which all of you, the pioneers and the sons of the pioneers, have had to show in peace, -- the qualities of hard, resolute work for a worthy end. South Dakota sent its regiment to the Philippines to serve in the war in which I served. The trouble with us was there was not enough war to go round--You had a big job to do, while ours was a mighty small one; but we did it, and we hope we did not show ourselves entirely unworthy of the men of the fought fields from'61 to '65. (Cheers and applause)

No one can come through your state without having it brought home to him how well off you are, how well you have done, not only in the things of the body but in the things of the mind. This is a state of farmers and stockgrowers, and in the southwest, of miners. There has been of late a higher average of production of wealth per head in this state than in any other state of the union. (Applause) Into such prosperity several different causes necessarily enter. In the first place of course nature has to favor us. If the hand of the Lord is heavy upon us, if drought comes, if the crops are smitten by disease, [-xx-] if the cattle, then the wit of man can only do a certain amount to avert the calamity. Legislation can do something, and bad legislation can stop it entirely; {bbut|but} most of it has to be done by the quality of the individual man. If I were addressing an assemblage of weaklings, of foolish men, of timid men, I would be addressing an assemblage that had failed instead of an assemblage that had succeeded. No law that the wit of man ever has framed or {everwill|ever will} frame [-xxx-] will make a timid, and [?weak?],and foolish man able to do the work that the hard-headed, stout-hearted, strong-thewed man can do. It is so everywhere in life. We all know of it in dealing with the man whose farm is next to ours, whose shop is next to ours,

[pb 67 425:0226]

the men whom we met {abd|and} contend with in any of the professions. Nothing can take the place of the individual's own quality. Every one at times needs help. Each of us should be only too {gladto|glad to} extend a helping hand in such a case; but in the long run the only help that really counts is the helping a man to help himself. You can lift up a man if he stumbles; if he lies down you cannot carry him. If you try to, it will not help him, and it will not help you. So, fundamentally, it must rest upon yourself to win success. As I said, law can do something; wise legislation, wise and honest administration of the government can do something. If you have [-xxxx-] bad laws, badly administered, they will spoil any prosperity. It is easy enough to get a bad law that will stop the whole business, but to get a good law is not so easy. And under a good law there will be little imperfections, and under any administration of the laws there will be little imperfections, there will be hitches here and there, there will be points on which you think you could make an improvement. {Edpecially|Especially} will you think that if you have never tried to make an improvement. It is awfully easy to sit outside and say how the man running the machine should do;[-x-]but it is not so easy to do it. This prosperity to which we have attained has been attained under a certain economic system, through carrying out [-[illegible]-] certain ideas in currency and the tariff. We cannot afford to reverse the system. Improvement can be made in it, In the tariff for instance, schedules are not sacred, and, as the needs of the nation change and shift[-[illegible]-] it will be necessary to change certain schedules to meet those shifting needs. But remember always that the mere fact that a given schedule could be changed with advantage, does not mean that it is possible at once to change it. Everything has got to be changed as part of the connected whole, and a change should not be made until the good of it is sufficient to counterbalance the certainty that there will be some dislocation, some trouble connected with making it. And above all the change should not be made, save as a matter of the reapplication of the details of the system without changing the principle of the system under which this country has so progressed. This is not a matter of politics, it is a matter of business. It is a matter of the business of all of us, the business of the republic, and I would ask that you approach it in the same spirit in which you approach any question affecting your own farms, your own [-[?own?]-] business[-xxxxxxxxxx-] enterprises ; and when it has all been done bear in mind ever what I said at the outset . something can be done by law; something has

[pb 68 425:0226]

been done by law. We could not have prospered as we have prospered during the last few years, unless we had had a good system of laws, and wise and upright administration of the laws. But law can do no more than give a brave, [-[illegible]-] honest and [-[illegible]-] intelligent man a chance to help himself.

Ultimately our success as a nation must depend upon the high average of citizenship of the individual.Here in the United States.we have won in the past and we shall win in the future because our citizens have in them the stuff out of which to get that high grade of citizenship; (Applause) because they [?have?] the strong body, the strong mind,and what counts for more than either body or mind -- character-- character, of which the three components are honesty, courage {abd|and} common sense. [+(+]Cheers and prolonged applause)
