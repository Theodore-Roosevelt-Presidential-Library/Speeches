My Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to catch a glimpse of you this evening, and I thank you for coming here to greet me. I only wish it had been my good luck to be able to spend a longer time in Arizona, but I was limited by the force of what I had to do elsewhere, so that having but the day I thought I could not spend it more wisely than in visiting what I am tempted to call the greatest scenic feature of the kind in the world-- the Grand Canyon of the Colorado. It has been a great pleasure to be here in Arizona. I have taken of course the keenest interest in your development and progress ever since I drew a quarter of my regiment from Arizona. (Applause)

There are surely here men who fought in the great war from '61 to '65, and those know what I think all of you will realize that no tie can be quite as close as the tie which binds a man to the others with whom he has marched, with whom he has lain in the {t renches|trenches}, with whom he has shared hardtacks [+(+]and was mighty glad to get them[+)+] and who have a claim on him that no other can have.

I have enjoyed greatly passing through the country, but what I have enjoyed most has been seeing the people. I know your people. I know your men and women, and I believe in them with all my heart.

A national measure that has recently been enacted by Congress will work good in many Rocky Mountain states and territories, but no where more than in Arizona; that is the national aid to irrigation. (Applause) Of the five experiments first undertaken by the national government the largest is to be undertaken in Arizona. (Applause) Government can do something by laws like that, but after all, my fellow citizens, men and women of Arizona, the determining factor in the success of any commonwealth must be the average citizenship of that commonwealth, just as the determining factor in the success of any man must be that man's own qualities. Law can do something. Honest, upright, fearless administration of the law can do more, but after all is said and done we need ever to keep before our minds that no law that the wit of man can devise ever made a fool wise, a coward brave, or

[pb 86 425:0369]

a weakling strong, or ever made a community prosperous unless the [?average?] citizenship of the community was such that it could prosper. (Applause) What law can do is to prevent injustice, and, so far as may be, to give to each man the chance to show the stuff that there is in him under the most favorable circumstances. (Applause) But if the stuff is not in him, you cannot get it out of him. I believe in the future of Arizona because I believe that you have the stuff in you, because I believe in your average citizenship. (Cheers and applause)
