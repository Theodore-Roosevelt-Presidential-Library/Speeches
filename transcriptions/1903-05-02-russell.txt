Mr. Senator; and you, My Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to me to come here into Kansas now, into the western part of the state, and see you. I thank you for your greeting of me. Wherever I have gone yesterday and to-day I have been greeted by two sets of Kansans whom it has given me peculiar pleasure to see-- the men of the Grand Army, the [-[illegible]-] men to whom this country owes more than it ever has owed to any other set of men since its foundation, the men who in the days of the civil war upheld the hands of[-xxxx-]Abraham Lincoln as they followed Grant, {andSherman|and Sherman}, and Sheridan, and Thomas to the ultimate victory[+.+] [-t-][+T+]o you first, to you who illustrate all the glory of the nation's past and who serve as a constant inspiration for the present and the future[+;+] and next to the future, to the children, I want to say how glad I am to see you all. Gentlemen and Ladies, while I like all that you have done and congratulate you upon your crops I congratulate you upon your children most. (Applause) I like your stock and I am glad it is being kept up. These children seem to be all right in quality and all right in [-xxxx xxxx-] quantity. (Applause) [-I [?guess?] Kansas is all right on the [illegible]-]

I have been struck as I have come through Kansas by much what struck me in Nebraska, in this prairie region, and that is the astonishing difference that has been made by the work of man in turning the prairie land into fertile cultivated regions. The growth of the {thees|trees}, the mere tree planting that has been done, is something wonderful. More and more in our country we are getting to understand the value of the woods, the value of the trees, In some of the older states and in some of the newer out to the west the problem is to preserve the forests. Here in Kansas you have been individually planting groves until the increase in the woodland is something astonishing, and I hope that soon we shall have a special governmental reserve in the southwest, so that the government will be able to do its part in testing what trees can best be grown in certain soils. In the next place, remembering how Kansas was when I first saw it some twenty years ago, I want to congratulate you upon the change that the alfalfa has made. It is a good thing when any state can get [-xxxx-][+more+] than one staple, a very good thing. There is not as much chance of disaster happening when this is the case. I congratulate you upon the growing diversity of your products, but most of all, my fellow citizens, do I congratulate you upon the type of manhood and womanhood that you are {produdng|producing} here, upon the work that is being done here. Of the work of many kinds perhaps I appreciate most the work that

[pb 41 425:0344]

is done by those who {havecharge|have charge} of the educational system. In the long run what counts is not soil, not climate, but the kind of stuff in the {m n|men}. who are to make the best of soil and climate.

Kansas could not have become what it is, could not have taken the place that [-[?she?]-][+it+] has taken, save [-xx xxxx-] because of the kind of blood that went into the making of[-[?our?]-][+[?its?]+] people. Kansas was founded by men who came here for an ideal, an ideal for which they were willing to struggle, and if need to die. Kansas has since then passed through the {fir e|fire} , the people of Kansas have seen and faced iron difficulties, and they have won out because they had the right temper, the right spirit, the right quality of mind and body and soul, that enabled them to win out, because they had the {ri ght|right} men and what is even more important-- the right women. Of course it is a mere truism to say that it is the home that counts. The average woman has, I think, a little the hardest lot in life, and every man is bound in honor to try to make it as easy as he can. It is the home that counts most, and the first requisite of a man's {be ing|being} a good citizen is that he shall be a good homemaker, a good father, a good husband, then a good neighbor, then a square and decent man in his dealings with the state, and I congratulate you here that you have had the two qualities-- the belief in decency, the belief in honor, the belief in patriotism, the love of country, and in the next place the strength, {t he|the} courage, the hardihood, which made the effort count. Every decent man, if he is going to count, has got to have that back of him. Patriotism did not count in '61 unless you had the fighting edge to back it up. So it is in citizenship; we need body, we {needmind|need mind}, we need most of all character--character, in which many elements enter but {threemost|three most} of all, the quality of decency, of honesty, the quality of courage, of strength, and finally the saving grace of common sense.

I believe in you with all my heart. I wish you well in the future and an ever increasing portion of the prosperity and the material and moral well-being which you now enjoy. (Cheers and applause)
