Ladies and Gentlemen:

I am glad to see you this evening. I have now traveled from the eastern well-nigh to the western border of your great and beautiful state, and much though I have always thought of it, I think I appreciate even better than ever before its great possibilities, its splendid agricultural future, and the change in industr[?i?]al development in more than one direction. I appreciate that more than ever before. There was one thing that I did not need to learn, and that is to appreciate your men and women. Your people I have known for a number of years. I lived out west in the short grass country myself,farther north in the Dakotas, when I ran a cattle herd up there, so I was fairly familiar with the stock business at that time. I want to say that when I began the business the cattle were all horns and tails. You have got a great improvement in livestock since I was in the business. These animals that I see along the track, I would have thought [-[illegible]-] came from the far east in my day. It makes me feel at home to be out here among you, and I want to thank the citizens of Sharon Springs in advance, because I am going to spend Sunday here. [-[illegible] [?Sunday night?] [illegible]-]. I want to thank you cordially for coming here, and especially to thank those who brought their children. (Cheers and applause.)
