Mr. Chairman and Gentlemen:

I wish to thank you most cordially not only for the beautiful token by which I shall always remember, if anything were necessary to make me remember, the pleasant lunch I have had with you to-day; but also I [-wish-][+want+] to thank you for the distinguished kindness and hospitality with which I have been treated here. It is indeed a pleasure to come here. I am the eighth generation born in New York City of my people, so I think I am entitled to say that I am indeed in the heart of the country here; for I feel that here in what used to be the west, but now is so near the middle that I am sure it is not far from the effete east, is where our people develop those characteristics which we naturally associate with the name American. (Applause)

The west is so purely a relative [-[illegible]-] term that you have to locate the man's individual abode before you know what he means by it. I remember that when I was in the cow country , one of the cow hands working for me came to me at the end of the season and said he would like his time as he was going away. I said " Where are you going?" He replied "I am going to spend the winter in the far east." "Well" I said" what do you mean by that, Norway or Nubia?" He said, "No, Duluth."

So to-day, in this country, we know no east or west, north or south, we merely know good Americans[-xxxxx-] wherever [-[?we meet it?]-].they may be. (Great applause)
