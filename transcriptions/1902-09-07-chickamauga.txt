Colonel, you have already introduced me to the officers of your regiment, two of whom for my good fortune served once in my regiment with me. [-[?With?]-] the others I served beside. I now wish through you to tell the enlisted men of the regiment what a pleasure it has been to see them this morning, and to say that I am glad to think as I look at them that they can be accepted as fair samples of the regular army (Applause). As you know, Colonel, I swear by the regulars (applause), and it made me feel proud to look at you. I enjoyed my ride with you and I wish it might have been longer (Laughter and applause). I want to add that the setting up exercises that I saw were the only setting up exercises in which I should [+have+] like[+d+] to [-[?have?]-] take[-[?n?]-] part (Applause). [-[?Colonel?]-], at the same time I want to make one remark in reference to the troop of state cavalry who were my escort down at Chattanooga. I looked at those and I felt that they were my men whom I had under me in the old days, and it was a pleasure to me to hear that they had just been taking a ten-days' march that was a real march, where they did the real work. I do not think very much of the soldiering that is gained only in the barracks and on the parade ground. I want to see a man able to take care of himself and able to handle himself under conditions somewhat akin to actual service. I wish to congratulate the officers and men of that troop of [+the+] state guard of Tennessee for the kind of work that they have been doing. And now good luck and Godspeed (Great applause).
