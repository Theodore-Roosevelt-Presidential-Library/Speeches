Mr. Mayor, and you my friends and fellow citizens, my fellow Americans:

It is a pleasure to be {withyou|with you} to-day.   I thank you for your greeting, and I thank you personally[+,+] Mr. Mayor[+,+] for the kindly and gracious words in which you have phrased that greeting[+;+] and while thanking you all for the way you have come out to meet me, I know you will not grudge my saying that I am particularly touched and pleased at having as a guard of honor the veterans of the Great War, the men who in the times that tried men's souls proved their truth by their endeavor; and second only to them my comrades of the lesser war.      It was not given to us to prove our [-[?metal?]-][+mettle+] in a great struggle.   All we could do was to show that we had the desire in us to follow  in your footsteps [-[?a?]-] if the need arose. (Applause)

I have been particularly pleased with an incident in connection with the presence of the veterans to-day.   It was a delight to shake hands with Father Boyle, carrying the button of the Loyal Legion, with Chaplain Wakely, just returned from the Philippines, and to see my old friend, the Rev. Dr. Sawyer,  marching in the ranks[+.+](applause) [-and-] I was glad to see them not only from the feeling of personal friendship but because it is a good thing to show that the church , that religion, morality, all we mean when we speak of {thoseterms|those terms}, [-that they-] are included in the service rendered by you and those like you[+,+] my comrades. (Applause)   The men who fought in the great war, whose example you and I {soughtto|sought to} emulate, [-the men who fought in the great war-] not only gave us lessons in soldiership, the lessons only to be applied in a great crisis, but they gave us lessons in sound citizenship.

Here in this community, built up by the industry, the business capacity of your people, it is well to remember the need of the qualities which make our material prosperity.   We want to remember that. We want to keep in mind the fact that our material well-being must stand as the foundation of the national well-being.   But we must remember also that the foundation is not the building; that the foundation by itself, the base by itself counts only with reference to the structure raised upon it.   When in 1861 [?mighty?] Abraham Lincoln sent you to war

[pb 165 424:0639]

for the Union, it was a {necessarything|necessary thing} that this country should have all of the material prosperity, that it should have been spread {ofer|over} forest and plain, that there should be in it the mills, and the railroads and the factories, that there should be wealth and what wealth could command at the [-[?adminix?]-] country's call; but the important thing was not the wealth, not the material well-being, but the men of the country ; the men, that was what counted, and you did the deed you did because  you felt deep in your hearts the knowledge that there was something more important than any material well-being.   You marched south, you gave up  all thoughts of ease, you showed yourselves willing to lay down your lives because in your souls was the lift to lofty things, and the spirit that the men of the great war showed in the time of war we must show in time of peace.   The qualities that make good citizenship in their elements are very much the  same qualities that made good soldiership.   There are exceptions of course, but as a rule the man who is a good soldier is a good {citizenp|citizen} because in every part of life you need the qualities of courage, of loyalty and capacity for comradeship, the spirit of honest dealing as between man and man, of common sense; these are the very qualities which the best soldier must inevitably have.   There are two lessons especially taught by the men who triumphed [-xxxx-] in the long contest from [-xx-] '61 to '65[+--+] [-t-][+T+]he lesson of the importance of the individual qualities and the lesson of brotherhood.   The gun was important but it was the man behind the gun that counted. (Applause)   You were drilled in different tactics from those [-under-][+[?in?]+] which the men who wore the blue and buff in the continental army of Washington were drilled, your weapons were different from theirs, but the spirit that drove you on was the same; and so now we of the younger generation, we who if called to war must fight in open order, not with the old elbow to elbow touch, but in open order, with smokeless-pow[+d+]er, high[+-+]power, small[+-+]caliber rifles[+,+] as different as those of our forefathers were from the crossbow, [-[illegible]-][+--+] we should make a poor fist of it if we did not have the same spirit back of us, the same spirit in us[+.+] [-t-][+T+]he qualities that make [pb 166 424:0640] a good soldier now [-[?thak?]-][+that+] made them in '61, [+that+] made them in '76, are fundamentally the same.   You have got to have the manhood in a man to make him able to play a man's part in the world.

And so now in civil life we have to face the problems of a highly complex industrial civilization; a much more complicated life than the life [?of?] our forefathers  led.   There is need for new laws; here and t[+h+]ere for new devices in the constitution; there is need for a certain shifting of the part that the state can play in the affairs of the individual, but in the last resort the qualities that made a man a good citizen in the days when the constitution was adopted under the [-xxx-] leadership of George Washington , in the days when the constitution and the country were preserved under the lead of Abraham Lincoln, those are the qualities that you need to make a good citizen now.   There is no patent device that will take the place of them; no law, no scheme of laws will avail the country if there isn't the high average of citizenship behind the law.

It is just as it was in war.   We need good weapons-it always irritates me to see a division of the national guard armed with black powder muskets.   I want to see all of our people with the best modern weapons(Applause) , because it is an outrage for a great and rich country like ours [-xx-] not to [-[?get?]-][+give+] the {Ameri can|American} who wears Uncle Sam's uniform, whether in the regulars, volunteers or national guard,[+--+] it is an outrage not to give him a weapon as good as that carried by any fighting man in the world. (Applause)   I want to see the best weapon given him, but the best weapon by itself is not anything like enough. If the man carrying the best weapon is no good he will be beaten by a good man with a {wlub|club}. (Laughter and applause)  You want a good weapon, but you need a good {men|man} to carry the weapon.   So we need good laws, a good constitution, but in the final resort what we need most is good citizenship; intelligent citizenship; the man who understands both his rights and his duties.   The man who only {taks|talks} of his rights and not of his duties is not a good citizen if he don't understand that the duties go hand in hand {wihh|with}[-xx-] the rights and if he does not under-

(Applause)
