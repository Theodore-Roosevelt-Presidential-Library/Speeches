Mr. Congressman; Mr. Mayor; and you, My Fellow Citizens, My Fellow Americans:

It is the very greatest pleasure {tmeet|to meet} you to-day, and I am profoundly touched and {plased|pleased} by your greeting. While I am going to come back again to speak to the public school children, let me just say at the outset that I think the flower strewing part of the greeting was something entirely new and very pretty. (Applause) It is a great pleasure to me to be back in Wyoming. I first came into your state 19 years ago -- came into it on a roundup wagon, (Cheers and applause) riding my own string of horses. I have known pretty well the part of your state from the Be[-[illegible]-][+l+]le Fourche across to the Clear Fork of the Powder and down to the Tongue and Rosebud.[-xx-] The ranch country I used to know pretty well in those days, and now I greet the people of a typical western region, a people of miners, stockgrowers, and farmers. It is in this region that dry farming has been carried to a greater extent and more prosperously than in any other part of Wyoming. Here you have your coal mines, cattle and sheep ranches, and in the neighborhood you have also developed the irrigation system along new lines. The Congressman was kind enough to allude to me as having a certain knowledge of your interests, and gentlemen, I can say with all conscientiousness that I have them closely at heart; and I wish now publicly to express my acknowledgments for the way in which your Senators and your Congressman have {coo perated|cooperated} with me in Washington. ({Cheersand|Cheers and} applause)

Men and women of Wyoming, it is a pleasure to address a community such as this, because in these communities I see embodied the spirit upon which we have to rely to make our country great -- the spirit of hard work, of self-reliant capacity each to shift for himself {andyet|and yet} of power to unite for the common benefit. {Wehave|We have} to show many different traits in order to win to the highest degree national success. There must be work {bythe|by the} Government; there must be wise legislation and upright and honest as w[-a-][+e+]ll as fearless and intelligent administration and legislation. But no making of laws, no administration of the law can take the place of the individual man's individual effort. Now le[-[?m?]-][+t+] me illustrate what I mean by referring to the problem in which you are not concretely interested here in this locality, but in which the entire west is interested from the broader standpoint -- the problem of irrigation. One of the best laws that Congress has passed of recent years was the irrigation law in [pb 111 425:0281] the enactment of which Mr. Mondell and your own Senators played so prominent a part. (Applause) I believe much good will come from it. That is, the Government will be able here and there to undertake enterprises which it cannot be expected that private capital will undertake, and the Government will be able to try experiments from the results of which private capital may be able to learn much. But though in that way Government can do a great deal, a great deal more can be done , as your citizens in this neighborhood have already done [+it+] by the individuals working separately or together on their own initiative and gradually developing the kind of irrigation that suits the special need of the locality. The west has been settled and built up primarily by the individual worth of the pioneer and the successor to the pioneer[-s-]. Law can do somewhat, and we must steadily [-xx-] endeavor to shift and adapt the laws to meet the new needs that from time to time arise. But after all , fundamentally, {wbat|what} counts is the honesty, the courage, the commonsense and the capacity for hard work of the average man. Nothing can take the place of [+those {qualitiesin|qualities in}+] the average man. There are other countries with institutions like those of our great republic, which, nevertheless, have failed to come up to the standard we have set because they did not have the man back of the law. Just as in battle it is the man behind the gun who counts most, so in civil life it is the average citizen back of the law who counts most.

To-day I have been particularly pleased with having the escort of the National Guard. Speaking to you of the National Guard I do not have to say how important it is that we should have good training and good arms. One of the laws passed by the last Congress which I was particularly glad to see passed was that providing for putting the National Guard on a thoroughly efficient basis, by which we will be able hereafter to secure the best type of arms for them. I want to see any man who wears, or may wear, Uncle Sam's uniform with the best weapon there is going. The best is none too good. (Applause) I want to give him the best weapon, but he has got to be a pretty good man besides that. If he is a poor stick himself, then he will be beaten by a good man with a club. I want to see him have a first-class rifle, and then I want to see a first-class man carrying the rifle. And I believe in you, because I think we can make the combination.

It is just so in civil life. We must have clean and decent government; we must have good laws; we must have decent {offidals|officials} to make and to execute [pb 112 425:0281] the laws. If we {dom|do} not it is our own fault. It is a {rpublic|republic}; it is a democracy; and it is our own fault if we do not get decent laws and decent administration of the laws. But all that a good law can do, all that good {administbation|administration} of the law can do, is to give each man a good chance to show the stuff there is in him. No law we ever have devised will make a coward brave, or a fool wise, or a weakling strong; and the fool, the coward, and the weakling will make but a poor fist of it under any system that can be invented. What the law can do is so to shape things that if a man has in him the root of righteousness, if he is decent, intelligent, hardy, brave, if he has forethought, and thrift, and energy, he will be able to turn these qualities to good account. That is what can safely be promised on behalf of the lawmaker and the administrator. More than that he cannot promise if he feels, as he ought to feel, that a man is just as much bound to keep a promise made on the stump as to keep one made off the stump.

I am particularly glad to be greeted by you, men and women of Wyoming, [-[?and?]-] to congratulate you upon the success of your mining and your stockgrowing and your farming, to say I believe with all my heart in the future of your state and in its products; but I want most of all and especially to congratulate you upon one product -- I am mighty glad to see the children. (Applause)

The public schools are the safeguard of our nation. This is literally a fact. Our nation in the future is going to do well, as I know it will do well, because of the training of the children in whose hands twenty years hence the destiny of the nation will lie. I believe emphatically in you people, therefore I am glad to see that your stock is being kept up. Your children seem all right in quality and all right in quantity. And glad though I am to be greeted by the older people, I want to in a special way express my obligations to the children gathered here.

In closing let me just repeat one thing. There is no royal road to prosperity any more than there is any [-xxxx-] royal road to good government. Good government does not come by any special genius or brilliancy. Good government comes from the average man showing the qualities which we recognize as making a good friend and good neighbor. Prosperity comes not by any juggling [-[?of?]-][+with+] the laws, not by any effort to show a smartness unaccompanied by moral sense[?,?] [pb 113 425:0282] it comes by the constant exercise on the part of the individual of those qualities which each of us recognizes as making a desirable citizen of our neighbor in private life. In their essence these qualities can be put under three heads. In the first place honesty, using the word in its [?broadest?] sense, decency, clean living, the quality that makes a man a good husband, the woman a good wife; [-[?the?]-] that makes men and women care for and bring up their children; that makes the man scrupulous to deal fairly and squarely by his neighbors; that makes him of the type with whom we are glad to have dealings in the business world, whose property we are glad to have adjoin ours, whom we recognize as a valuable citizen of the community -- you have got to have that first-- decency, the spirit of fair dealing, the spirit that makes a man a help to others in the community. It is not enough. It is good to lie at the base, but it is not enough. In addition to that you need other qualities. I do not care how decent the man is, if he is afraid he is [illegible] no good. I do not care how kind, how friendly, how pleasant, how nice to deal with, if he will flinch when the time comes, if he is a weakling, you cannot trust him, you can do nothing with him. (Applause) I guess from the sound that there are some gentlemen here who have been on the round-up; and when, on a fall morning, a horse arches his back, you want to feel sure that the puncher that gets on him is not only a nice man but that he knows how to ride. (Applause) It is so all through, in every branch of life. I want to see not only a good man, but [+also+] a man. It is good to be brave, it is good to have courage, it is good to have hardihood, but he has got to have the spirit that when he meets difficulties he does not feel badly and want to go home, but sets out to overcome them and trample them down and win success in spite of them. You have got to have decency and courage too, and those two qualities are not enough. I do not care how honest a man is and how brave he is, if he is a natural born fool you cannot do anything with him. You must have in addition the quality of common sense, of intelligence. You must have the faculty [-[illegible]-] not merely of doing right and of being fearless and strong, but of knowing how to handle yourself. You have to have the three qualities, courage, honesty, and common sense. And, gentlemen and ladies, I believe in Wyoming, I believe in the west, because your people here have got just those three qualiti[-[illegible]-]es. (Great applause)
