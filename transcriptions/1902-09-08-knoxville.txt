Mr. Gibson, my fellow countrymen: I wish to thank you for having come out in this weather. Somebody asked me [-if-] when it was raining whether I would come up here anyway. I told them I guessed that if you could stand it I could (Laughter and applause). I want to say how much I appreciate  your having come out to see me. I have had proof positive that the national guard are not fair-weather soldiers (Applause).

It has been an inspiration to come through east Tennessee to-day as I have come through. I am sorry for my sake that the rain should have come at this particular moment, but you needed it and I am glad you have got it (Applause). Yesterday I spent the time going over the great battle field[+s+] of Chickamauga and Chattanooga, partly within the boundaries of your own state, partly within the boundaries of my mother's state, Georgia. Now I come here along the upper waters of the Tennessee to the seat of the first community settled by people of our stock on the waters which flowed into the Gulf of Mexico. It was six years before the outbreak of the Revolutionary War that the first little settlement out of which grew the great state of Tennessee was founded. And it was here in this region that the [-xxxxx-] mounted riflemen gathered under the lead of Sevier - the shaft in commemoration of whom you see there - to go across the mountains, and strike at King's Mountain the decisive blow which in the end freed the southern states in the Revolutionary War. Right from the beginning the Tennesseeans showed themselves typical Americans in the way in which they proved their ability to contend in time of war for their rights and to handle themselves well in time of peace. [+And from Tennessee came the great Admiral of the Civil War - Farragut.+] Great has been Tennessee's share in the leadership of the country alike in war and in peace, because while you have been a great {k agricultural|agricultural} state, while you are now rapidly becoming in this section one of the greatest industrial centers of the entire country, you have remembered {thr oughout|throughout} that great though it is to produce the works of man, it is greater still to produce man himself (Applause). It is a great thing to have the factory, the mine, the railroad, but greatest of all is it to have a high average of citizenship in the community; and that must mean that you have men with the power to [pb 41 424:0702] practice the gentler virtues and the rougher virtues at [?need?] too. Tennessee has won her place. The typical Tennesseean stands [-xxx-] as embodying so much which we are glad to call typically American, because the Tennessean has never shrunk from the roughness of life. The men who first came here and on the head waters of the tributaries of the Tennessee River built the little palisaded forts from which they defeated the Chickamauga Indians - those men did not come here in search of ease. They came here because this great and rich and beautiful territory opened to them the chance of a victory worth having if they proved themselves men enough to wrest that victory from the forces of savage man and savage nature. I would not preach to any man for whom I cared the snap of my [-fixxxx-] finger the gospel of ease (Applause). The man who is to be envied is the man who works. He may work in one way or he may work in another. I care not the way in which he works if his work is worth doing and well done (Applause). I care not whether a man work with his hands or his head; I care not what may be the ,line [-inxwhichxxx-] along which he has chosen to do his work; what I feel that we have the right to ask is, is it worth doing and is it done well That work may take many forms. At Chickamauga, at Missionary Ridge, at Lookout Mountain yesterday I saw the scene of the desperate fighting of the men who wore the blue and the men who wore the [?grey?] , and we are to be congratulated that we have such examples before us. (The President's speech was here interrupted by a heavy downpour of rain). He closed by saying: I want to say that I am very much obliged to you for coming out to see me. I hope you are half as glad to see me as I am to see you. [+(Great applause)+]
