My {Fiends|Friends} and Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to have the chance of saying a word to you this afternoon, and to address this great gathering of the men and women of this beautiful state . Here as everywhere I see in the audience men who wear the button which shows that in the great war they fought when Abraham Lincoln summoned them to fight; and I have also seen men here who [-[illegible]-] were my own comrades in the lesser war.

Those men by their lives and their deeds teach us what it behooves us each and all to learn, and one lesson above all others -- the lesson of brotherhood; the lesson of the substantial unity of all Americans. They fought to destroy forever the {pirit|spirit} of sectional hatred. Let us learn from them that the arch foe of all that is best in American life is the man who seeks to stir one American against his fellows, the man who appeals in a spirit of hatred to induce one set of Americans to stand opposed to their fellows, whether he makes that appeal in the hope of exciting section against section, creed against creed, or class against class. Of all men, the man most to beware of {isthe|is the} man who will try to persuade any of our people that it is to their interest to act wrongly [-xxxxxx-] toward any others . If any man seeks to persuade any of our people in their fancied self-interest to do {somet ing|something} against the well-being of their fellows, it is a safe thing to keep in mind that the man who will try to get you to follow him in wrong doing on the plea that it will be to your interest to do so, will if the chance comes wrong you in his turn, because it is in his interest. The only safe rule to follow in public life as in private life is to act squarely and fairly by your fellows, and to remember that whoever preaches another line of conduct as he preaches to you to do wrong for your fancied gain will for his {interst|interest} wrong you in his turn if the chance arises.

You, my comrade there, and those like you in the great war fought to establish in this country a government not of license but of liberty under, by, and through the law; liberty which [-will-][+should+] be guaranteed to every man, not because the man {wasrich|was rich}, not because the man was poor, but because [pb 17 425:0532] as long as he was a {aquare|square} man, rich or poor, of whatever occupation, of whatever creed, he was entitled to not only the protection of the law but the good-will of his fellow citizens. That is what our government means. Our government is not and can[-xxx-] never be made successfully a government by class or by section; and we are false to the principles of the men who in 1776 founded it, of the men who in the days of {Ab aham|Abraham} Lincoln preserved it, if we do not act upon their fundamental principles of duty, if we do not act on the theory that each man is entitled to be judged {o n|on} his worth as a man, to be guaranteed his rights against all others and to our protection {frombeing|from being} wronged by all others; it makes no difference whether the {wro ng|wrong} takes the form of a crime of greed and cunning, or whether it takes the form of physical violence. In either case the offense against the law is an offense against the whole community, and in the interest of the whole community it must be put down. While any of our people who violate the law are to be esteemed not only wicked but foolish, the most foolish of our people is the man of means who violates it; because his entire well-being, his entire success in life, the security of his belongings, depends upon the protection which the law throws around him as a mantle.

It has been a great pleasure to meet you to-day. I have enjoyed being in your great state; but most of all I have enjoyed seeing the men and women of the state. I started on this trip a good American. I think I am coming [-xxxx-] back an even better American, the more I see of my country and my countrymen. (Cheers and applause) I have not got much to {teahh|teach} you; but I have a good deal to learn from you; and I have tried faithfully to learn it. I believe {inthe|in the} future of this country, because I believe the average citizen has in him the capacity for courage, for decency, and for common sense. (Cheers and applause[?)?]
