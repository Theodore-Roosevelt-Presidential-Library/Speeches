Senator Burton, and you,my {friands|friends} and fellow-citizens:

It is a pleasure to come to the home of Senator Burton in which he has spent a quarter of a century of his life, and to be greeted by his neighbors[+,+] and I thank you, Senator, and {OI|I} thank you all for the courtesy you have extended to me. I want to thank here for the first time a body of your people before I thank the Grand Army,[+--+] the small people, those little girls who greeted me and who threw the flowers down as we passed. I am {verymuch|very much} obliged to you and I greet you and I greet all the children here to-day. And [+I know+] you men of the Grand Army[+,+] do not mind my having put them first. They are the future, and fortunate it is for the generation of the future when they live in a state, in a country, in a city, where they have in evidence before them the organization of the men to whom we owe it that we have a country. Great though the deeds were that you did in the past, great though their effect was, nothing can be greater than the example you thus gave to those who are coming up, who are to be the Americans of to-morrow. I want to thank the band and say I recognized in the tune the war tune of the 20th Kansas, and I heard it in the trenches myself.

It has been a great pleasure to come through Kansas. I know Kansas well. It is the first time I have been here as President, but I have been here often before, and you are partly responsible for having me here as President.(Applause.) [-In fact, Senator Burton, you may remember when you and the other Kansas delegates insisted upon my taking the Vice Presidency and I did not want it. (Senator Burton: "We wanted you to have it as a stepping stone.")-] I am glad to have the chance of {meetin g|meeting} you, the men and women of this great state. I congratulate you upon the material products of the state, upon your crops, upon your industries of every kind. I have been through your cities that are railroad centers and that are growing to be centers of manufactures; but after all the prime product of any state must be its men and women. That is what {co unts|counts}; and those men and women have got to have two sets of [-xxxx-] qualities if they are to make their state and their nation a source of pride for the ages[-[illegible]-] [-t-][+T+]wo sets, the

strength and the sweetness that have been held essential from the days when the son of Jesse became King of [-xxx-] Israel. Strength! There is no place in this world for those who are [-x-] afraid, for those who shirk life's difficulties, for those who have not daring, hardihood, resolution. You who came here and made this state great, you could have done nothing if it had not been that you had cool heads, stout [-xxxx-] hands, strong hearts. We must have the virile , [-[illegible]-][+manly+] virtues if we are to make of this nation the success which we shall make of it, and with those qualities must go others; must go self-mastery and self[-xxx-][+r+]estraint, the spirit that makes a man deal [-xxxx-] fair and square with his neighbors. We need [-[illegible]-] virtue, decency, honesty, the spirit of fair dealing, the spirit of obedience to law, the spirit that makes a good husband, a good father, and a good neighbor, a decent man in his relations with the rest of the world. We must have them, and then we must have something more. The virtue that {stay s|stays} at home in its own parlor and regrets that the world is not better, does not count. The good man who is afraid is of no use. With virtue, with decency, must go hand in hand the quality of manliness, the quality that makes a man able to hold his own in the hurly-burly of life.

You, my {camrades|comrades}, in the great war demanded and had to demand in a soldier first that he should have the power of devotion to the flag, the power of devotion to the country[+.+] [-h-][+H+]e had to be patriotic. Patriotism, that was the first quality, but you did not care how patriotic a man was if he ran away he was no good. In addition to being patriotic he had to possess the fighting edge. He had to be the kind of man that when the order came to move [-[?he?]-] would move in the right direction. It is so in citizenship. We must have patriotism and decency and honesty. If a man has not [-[?got?]-] the root of decent conduct in him then the abler he is and the braver he is the more dangerous he is to the community at large. He is purely a kind of civic wild beast and you need to hunt him down and hunt him out of the community.

[pb 34 425:0341]

There is not in all America a more dangerous trait than the deification of mere smartness unaccompanied by any [-[illegible]-][+[?sense?]+] of moral responsibility. All of us know of some man[+of+] whom our foolish neighbors , for we all of us have occasionally foolish neighbors, will say, [+"+]well maybe he is not quite straight, but he is [?dreffful?] smart.[+"+] The man of whom that can be said and the man who says it are both poor citizens, and we shall not make of this great republic and commonwealth what it must [-xxxx-] be made until we join to hunt down the successful {rascaljust|rascal just} as eagerly as we hunt down the unsuccessful rascal.

We have got to have first, the spirit of decency, and joined with that the spirit of manliness, the spirit that makes a man able to hold his own in military strife or in the hard, rough work of the world. Our soldiers in '61 had not only to possess good intentions [+but the power to put them into effect+] to make themselves felt; and the men who came out here [-x-] to carve this great and beautiful state out of the prairie land had to possess not merely the desire to do well, but the power, the rugged strength to do well. Kansas is great to-day, and our country is great to-day [-xxxx-] because hitherto we have been able to combine the two qualities [-xx-] [-xxxx-] in our citizenship[+.+] [-t-][+T+]he quality of striving after decency and the quality of [-[illegible]-] {strivingse|striving so} as to make our force {efficien ly|efficiently} felt. In citizenship we need strong bodies, and even more than strength of body we need strength of mind, and most of all we need what counts for more than body and more than mind-character[+--+] Character, into which many elements enter, but three especially-the {elementSof|elements of} honesty[+,+] of courage and the saving grace of common sense. (Cheers and applause.)
