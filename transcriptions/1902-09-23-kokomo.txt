My fellow citizens:

I thank you greatly for having come out to greet me today; and I am glad I was able to stop at your beautiful town and to see here one of the many industrial centers which in the aggregate are working out the mighty destiny of this nation. Material prosperity isn't everything; but it is the foundation upon which we must build the larger life. Here in your community you have great natural resources. Here in Indiana you have in the earth and under the earth agencies which can be developed for the good of mankind, but after all we must ever remember that the resource is only the opportunity, and that in order to succeed you need the man to take advantage of the opportunity. The reason you win out here, the reason our people as a whole win out, is because we have the men to take advantage of the natural resources of the country. We are very prosperous, and the prosperity is not ephemeral. Of course it will go up and down somewhat. The waves will break and recede and advance, but the tide will go steadily on. I would preach to you ever the creed of optimism, the creed of hope. Indiana, whenever we have had a war, has sent her sons with lavish freedom to the war. I earnestly hope and believe that never in our time again will we see a war; but you need fundamentally for victory in peace the same qualities that your sons showed on the armed field. No man ever won a fight yet by thinking he was going to lose it. The prophets of evil, the apostles of despair are but poor guides for a country like ours. We shall win, not by looking down, but by looking up; not by distrusting ourselves, but by knowing that we have it in us to wrest victory from hostile forces. You, my friend and comrade, over there, you, who fought in the big war, you and your comrades who were with you, won because you believed in the republic. (Cheers and applause) And we are going to work out our destiny in this land because we believe in the republic; because if we were betting men, we would bet on the continent and not against it. (Cheers and applause) We are a nation with a continent for a [pb 61 424:0749] footstool; it {grapps|grasps} an ocean in each hand. We have big things to do of course, and we are big enough to do them. (Applause) Difficulties in the way[+?+] Yes, there are difficulties in the way. Dangers[+?+] Yes. You faced dangers from '61 to '65, but you did not run away; you overcame them, and that is why we are all of us here proud of our birthright, our heritage as Americans. We are here because our forefathers believed greatly in this country, and it behooves us to show that we have the same faith in us. As you believed in time of war, so we believe in time of peace. There will be difficulties, but we shall treat them as something to overcome, not to shirk. There will be dangers, and we will face them as you faced the dangers of the stricken field. We preach the gospel of hope; of hope, that you, the men of the West so signally illustrate in all your lives. No American, and least of all a westerner has any business to be a pessimist. We have got a continent here. We have just begun to develop it, and if we cannot go on and continue to make it prosperous we are made of poor stuff, that is all. I believe in the physical advantages which underlie our prosperity, but I believe more in the qualities which make our people able to take advantage of {thosequalities|those qualities}. I believe that they have the intellect, the physical strength, and finally that which counts more than [-phy-] intellect, more than physical strength, that they have character—Character[+,+] into which [-th[illegible]-] many components enter, but these three above all: Character which is made up mainly of courage, of honesty and of the saving grace of common sense.

In thanking all of you, and thanking my comrades, the men of the national guard and my comrades of the regulars, I want to thank especially the veterans of the great war for having come out to greet me, and I know you will none of you grudge them. (Cheers and applause.)
