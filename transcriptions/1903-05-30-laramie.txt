Mr. Mayor; and you, My Fellow Citizens; Men and Women of Wyoming:

It is indeed a {ple asure|pleasure} to be here in your beautiful state on this beautiful day. I do not feel like a stranger among you. It {istwenty|is twenty} years since I first came into Wyoming. At that time I was in the cow business myself. (Applause) I know your people well. The Mayor just said to me he was glad I [-am-][+was+] in sympathy with the west. I told him that sympathy was {thewrong|the wrong} word; that I did not sympathize with the west; I admired it.

Here in this state I am particularly glad to have the chance of speaking from the State University building. Nothing has delighted me more during the twenty-five years that I have known the west than to see the rapidity with which together with the material development of the country hand in hand with it has gone the building up of the opportunities for leading a higher life, without which material development by itself must always count for so little.

Before going on I want to thank the Black Hills Lodge of the Locomotive Firemen for the gift tendered through the Mayor from them to me and to say that I wish I were able to thank them in person. For the last two months and for the next week I have owed and shall owe every hour and every day my safety to the care of the railroad men. I have appreciated deeply all that they have done for me, and appreciate very greatly the spirit that prompts them to remember me in the fashion they have.

I want also to say a special word of greeting on this day of all others, on Memorial Day, to the veterans of the Grand Army of the Republic, the men to whom we owe it that there is to-day a country [-xx xxx-] at all, and that to-day the President of the United States can go across this continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific, can go from the Gulf to the border line that separates us on the north from ou[-t-][+r+] Canadian brothers, and everywhere find himself at home, everywhere find himself among his own people. After all there is but little I can preach that you cannot yourselves learn from what these men of the Grand Army have practiced; and it is practice that counts[-[?;?]-]. Precept is very good; but one ounce of performance is worth a ton of promise and exhortation.
