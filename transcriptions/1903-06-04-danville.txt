My Fellow Countrymen; Men and Women of Illinois:

It is a great pleasure to come here this afternoon and say a word or two to you; and it is a double pleasure to come here to the home of the next {Speakerof|Speaker of} the House of Rep[+resentatives. (Cheers and applause)+] I wish to say now that the way in which Mr. Cannon was chosen, for chosen he has been, Speaker of the next House of Representatives, augured well for American public life. Mr. Cannon has had to play during his long service in Congress the most useful part that any member of Congress can play, and[-[illegible]-] yet [-xxx-] preeminently one which offhand would be said to militate against the chances of his further advancement. For Mr. Cannon has had to protect the interest of the nation, the {interrst|interest} of the people as a whole, from the improper demands of localities and individuals. If Mr. Cannon had been thinking of his own future merely, he would have been warranted in supposing that a service to the public at large at the expense of particular concrete interests, would have been forgotten by the public and remembered only by the individuals {unfavorb ly|unfavorably} affected. More than that -- Mr. Cannon not merely had to put a stop to things that were improper, but to set his face against the expenditures of money which would have been right enough in themselves except that we could not afford it; so that he incurred the displeasure not merely of the men who asked what was improper, but of those who asked what we all should have been glad to give if the purse {if|of} the nation {wese|were} large enough. It reflects well not merely on him but upon all our people in public life, that those deeds of his should prove not an obstacle to his advancement, but the method of making him, without a canvass on his part, without any intrigue or pressure, the choice of his party in Congress for the {seco nd|second} highest position in the active political service of the nation. (Applause)

I am very, very glad to have seen you. I believe in you and wish you well. The more I have been through this {cuntry|country} the more I feel that we are justified in an absolute optimism as to its future. I believe in {t e|the} country {becauseof|because of} its resources; I believe in it infinitely more because of the character of its citizenship, and the country is to be made successful because of the character of the men and women in it. It is a great thing to have the natural resources that we have; but the greatest thing is to have the men and women to take advantage [pb 68 425:0557] of those resources. {Weshall|We shall} go on as a nation; we [-xxxxxxxx-][+shall {succee d|succeed}+] as a nation, because the average standard of our citizenship will be kept to what it is.

In thanking all of you I wish especially to thank the men of the Grand Army, the men to whom we owe it that we have a nation to be proud of to-day; and I wish also to[+t+]hank the {officersand|officers and} men of the national guard[+.+] As a nation we must ever keep in mind the debt we owe to the regular army of the United States and to the national guard of the several states; {andNnothing|and nothing} can be done better than to show in every way by our actions that we support them. In greeting you I want to greet especially those who come carrying children[+.+] I have been pleased traveling through Illinois to see that apparently your children are all right in quality and in quantity. I like your stock and I want to see it kept up. It is a particular pleasure to me to have had even this glimpse of you. I am glad that [-[illegible]-] the last place in which I have spoken in Illinois [-[illegible]-] should be in the home of Mr. [-[?Joseph?]-] Cannon. I believe in you with all my heart; and I believe in the success of our ;people. I believe that our people will rise in the future to the glorious past of the men of the days of Lincoln, because I believe that the average American citizen has in him the qualities of courage, of decency, and of {commonsense|common sense}, which [+we+] must [-xxxx-] have as the foundation, as the cornerstone, of the republic. (Cheers and applause)
