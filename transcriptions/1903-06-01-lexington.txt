My Friends and Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to see you this evening and to be greeted by you. I have been across the continent from the Atlantic to the Pacific, and am about half way on my return. The one thing that has struck me more than anything else on the whole trip has been the essential unity of our people. Wherever I have been, to whatever audiences I have spoken, I have found them responsive to the same appeal for the same ideals, and the reason is that a good American is a good American no matter what part of the country he is in. (Applause) I have enjoyed to the full this trip. I think I started out a middling good American, but I am coming back a better American than I started. How any man can fail to believe in this country and this country's future I do not see, if he knows anything about the country. The more I have been over it and the more I have seen of our people the surer I am that whatever our temporary {troubleshere|troubles here} and there at different times may be, we are going to work out of them [-a-] all right simply because the average citizen has got in him the qualities of decency, courage and common sense, and when you have got those three attributes in a man or in a nation you are going to come very near getting out all right in the end.

(Cheers and applause)
