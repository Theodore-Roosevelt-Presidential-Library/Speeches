My fellow Citizens:

In greeting you all, let me first say a special word of greeting to the veterans of the great war, to the men without whom we would not have any country here of which to be proud; and next to my own comrades, the men of the National Guard, the men some of whom served in the Philippine[-x-] Islands. All that I can say of ourselves, the men of the lesser war, is that we tried to follow in the footsteps of the men of the great war.

I greet you all. I feel at home here in North Dakota. It was twenty-three years ago that I first came to your state, when it was then a territory. I know you well. I congratulate you upon upon all you do in wheat [-raising-][+growing+] and stock raising, but above all,in the kind of men and women you grow. (Applause) And here, with your normal and your high schools, whose professor I have just met, I see you are preparing for the next crop -- the children. (Applause)

[note: "raising" after "wheat" is x-ed out on the typewriter and "growing" is typed above it.]
[note: Hand marks that do not clearly change the words: a stroke over the letter after "war" (before the comma) in the first paragraph, and a stroke over the "w" of "know". After "Philippine" an x is typed over a letter (apparently s), cancelling it and "in" after "above all," is typed over another letter.]
[note: The capital R of "Roosevelt" in the heading is set below the line ("President Roosevelt").]
[note: Third look: at native resolution the last letter of "Philippine[-x-]" is a heavy x struck over another letter, so the word as corrected reads "Philippine".]
