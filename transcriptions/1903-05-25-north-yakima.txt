Mr. Mayor; and you, My Fellow Americans; Men and Women of this great state:

It is [-[illegible]-][+with+] the great est pleasure [+that I+] [-[illegible]-] come here this morning to greet you. I wish to say as I generally do at these meetings just a word or two of special greeting to certain Bodies of your citizens; first and foremost to the men of the great war, the veterans of the Grand Army. Out here generally I feel that after all I have got rather less to preach than to learn, because I think you have practiced pretty well what I am preaching[+.+] [-b-][+B+]ut that is [-xxxxxxxxx-] [+especially+] true of the men of the Grand {Amry|Army}. For in peace and in war they by their lives gave us the example [-xx xxxx xx-] which we must try ourselves to equal[+.+] [-a-][+A+]fter thanking them I wish to thank my own comrades , the men of the Spanish-American war. (Cheers and applause) You and I, my comrades of that war, tried to show that we had not forgotten the lesson taught us by the men of '61 to '65, the men of the great years of Abraham Lincoln. It pleased me particularly to pass by in you[+[?r?]+] street here [-[?the monument?]-] the monument raised to those who in the Philippines {gavethe|gave the} greatest possible proof of devotion to their country when they laid down their lives for it. (Applause)

Having thanked both of you, the elder men and the younger men, let say a word of special greeting on behalf of the children. I am glad to see throughout all this count ry the way in which you are providing for the real greatness, the real welfare of the nation in the future, by the training that you are giving to the children, the care that you are taking with your schools.- the public schools, the high schools, normal {sdools|schools}, academies of every kind; because nothing that can be done by this generation will in the end avail anything if the next generation is not of the quality to take advantage of it.

Coming here into this valley in this region which you have made blossom like a rose [-xx-] I am inevitably impelled to think of the greatest instrument in bringing about that fertility -- irrigation. It seems to me that the last Congress when it passed the national irrigation act marked the beginning of a policy more important to this country's internal devel opment than any other [-xxxxxxxx-] since the homestead law was first put upon the national statute books. Of course it always takes time; in the first place to get public opinion up to the point of enacting such a law, and the next place to get the law fairly under way in order that the benefits to accrue from it shall accrue; but a beginning ha[?s?] [+been+]
[pb 95 425:0473] made; the law has been put upon the statute books, and certain works in a sense ex perimental have been started, {andby|and by} the aid of the national governme nt cooperating with the state governments and with individuals and associations of individuals we shall see [+in+] the so-called arid and semi-arid regions a development during the next fifty years literally [-xxxxxxxxx-] unparalleled . You know, {ladiesand|ladies and} gentlemen, the old proverb that all that any place needs is good {sod ety|society} and water. You have got the good society, you have got the water, only [-it-][+the water+] is not always distributed as it ought to be. What is necessary to distribute that water [-both in [illegible] and in time, that-] is to get it out by the irrigati on ditches and ultimately to have the great storage reservoirs which will enable us to [-xxx-] [+take+] the waters that go to waste at a season when we do not need them and store them so that they can be used at a season when we do need them. When you come into a new country there is always a period during which the people have to adjust {themselvesto|themselves to} the new needs. It was sometime before the national legislature could understand that the same reasons which make it proper for the national government to undertake the improvement of the rivers along their lower course[+s+] in the humid regions make it even more incumbent upon the national government to do its part toward securing the right use of those {rivess|rivers} in their upper courses in the arid region[+s+]. The government has now entered upon that policy and very {geat|great} good will come from it.

I cannot too often say, my friends, and yet here in this community I think it is almost unnecessary for me to say it[+,+] that when all is said and do ne, when everything that government can do has been {accompliahed|accomplished}, it remains true that the chief factor in the success of any community must be the average citizenship of that community. Just exactly as in the civil war and in our own lesser war, the Spanish war, after you had the weapon and the uniform and the training the chief factor was the man behind the gun. Every one of us who has ever served has known [-certain men-][+the man+] to whom you could give a weapon or a uniform and train [-them-][+him+] as much as you wanted to and after it was all over he was not wor th anything because he did not have the stuff in him; and if the stuff was not :in him you could not get it out of him. It is the man behind the gun that counts in battle; the battle is finally won, the war is finally won, by the side which has as its average soldier the man of courage, of self-reliance, who knows how to ob ey and how to act when he has no chance to get {ordrrs|orders}, the man who has it in him t .o wrest victory
[pb 96 425:0473] out of defeat, who has it in him to accept each obstacle simply as a spur to fresh and greater efforts. If we have got that type we [-will-][+shall+] win; and if we [-xxx-] have not we [-[illegible]-][+shall+] lose. So in citizenship all that the wisest law can do, all that the best administration of the law can do, is to give each man the chance under favorable conditions to do the best he can for himself . Bad laws , corrupt and evil administration[-s-] of the laws can spoil that chance readily enough, but after we have gotten all that can be got by the best laws we then come down to the fact that the man's own persever[-e-][+a+]nce, resolution, hardihood, capacity to dare and to endure, capacity for self-command and self-restraint, capacity to carry to the end whatever he has started out to do-- those qualities must be the decisive [-xxxxxxxxx-] factors in success in civil life exactly as they are the decisive factors in success in military life. There is no royal road to success in life anywhere, not as a soldier, and not as a private citizen. All of you saw in the great war just as we did, my comrades, in the lesser war, men who were very anxious to be heroes if they did not have to go through the preliminary trouble. The man that made [-xx-] a good soldier as a rule was the man that did his duty as it came up -- the man that dug kitchen sinks when the time came to dig them, and policed the camp and did not fall out on the march, who did whatever work it was that he was set to do-- that man was usually the type of man upon whom you could depend[+,+] when the crisis of a fight came[+,+] to stay put. It is so in citizenship. There is no royal road to success in government, to success in private life. We need to exercise not genius, not brilliancy, but the humdrum, work-a-day qua lities that wear best in actual life now and that have worn be st since history began.

We need strong bodies; even more than that we need strong minds; more than even body or mind we need character; character, into which many elements enter but three especially-- hone sty first, courage and hardihood next, and finally the saving grace of co mmon sense. I believe in your future; I believe in the future of this entire country, because I believe that the average American of to-day has as his fathers had before him and as his sons will have after him just those three qualities; the quality of dec ency, the quality of courage, and the quality of sane and healthy common sense. (Cheers {nand|and} applause)
