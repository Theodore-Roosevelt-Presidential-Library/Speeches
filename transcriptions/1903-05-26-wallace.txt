Senator Heyburn; and you, My Fellow Americans; Men and Women of Idaho:

It is a great pleasure to me to be within the bounds of your state. In greeting all of you I shall, following the custom that I am happy to say I have been able to follow throughout the northwest, say a special word of greeting to two bodies of your citizens; first of all to the men of the Grand Army, to the men who in the civil war from {161|'61} to '65 proved their truth on war's red touchstone. I greet you not merely because of what you did in war, not merely because you left us a reunited country, a country in which wherever the President goes from the {Atalntic|Atlantic} to the Pacific, from the Gulf to the Canadian boundary, he can feel at home, but because you left us by your deeds imperishable memories as to how we should conduct ourselves now; and most vital of all you left us the memory of brotherhood and all that brotherhood means-- brotherhood first, my comrades, with the gallant men against whom you fought, the {gallantmen|gallant men} in gray, who and whose sons are now as straight Americans as any of the rest of us. (Applause) Wherever I go if I speak to a body of Union veterans I speak to men who[-[?m?]-] I know will be first to respond in generous tribute to the men of the south, to the me n against whom they fought. Now I wish to say a special {wordof|word of} greeting to my own comrades, the men of the Spanish-American war. In that war we had one difficulty from which you of the big war were wholly free; in our case there was not quite enough war to go around. At the same time those who [-campaigned-][+hiked+] in the Philippi[+n+]es for a year had a good experience. I want also to say a word of greeting to the representatives of the university who are here. In traveling through these great states, great in the present and infinitely greater because of the assured [+future+] that looms great before us[+,+] nothing has pleased me and impressed me more than the way in which amidst the {budy|busy} life of exploiting the material resources of the present such attention is paid to the generation that is to grow up in the future. Everywhere I have been greeted by the school children, and I think you all know I am peculiarly glad to see them. I want to congratulate Idaho on their quality and quantity. (Applause)

I have but one word to say to the children. I believe in play and I believe
[pb 106 425:0478] in work. Play hard while you play and when you work don't play at all.As I said it has been a great pleasure wherever I went to meet the evidences of the care paid through your schools, through your colleges, to the welfare of the next generation. We must have material well-being as a base, as a foundation. Unless we have good {busim ss|business} men, hard workers, men who can get the utmost out of our resources in mines, in lumber, in agriculture, in grazing, in transportation, in every branch of active work-- unless we have that capacity the nation cannot go on, but the capacity is not by itself enough. On it we must build the superstructure of the broader and the higher life; there must be the chance for mental and spiritual growth, in addition to caring for the things of the body, otherwise we cannot develop our life as it should be developed. I congratulate you upon the care you have given both to the elementary education and to the higher education in this state; and to the students of the university I have just this to say[?!?] Much has been given to you and from you rightly we will exact much in return. You {havehad|have had} special advantages in the way of education, in the way of training, and it behooves you to show that they have not been wasted, that the state has been justified in bestowing them upon you. You can make no return to your alma mater, to your college, save in one way; you can m k e return for the training and the education you have received in the college by the kind of service you render to the state and the public as you grow up. That is the only way in which you can make a return and that return we have a right to expect of you. We have a right to expect from you the highest type of service that can be rendered by American citizens.

I come into a state with great natural advantages, but with advantages which need absolutely hard and intelligent work that they may be developed. In this state you have mines and forests, agricultural regions, pastoral regions, there are regions to which irrigation will bring advantages, but in this climate and at this end of the state I shall not speak of irrigation. You have had these great advantages. They would have counted for less than nothing if you had not had the type of manhood and womanhood among your citizens which [-xxxx-] would have enabled you to make the advantages show in reality as such. Exactly as in war it is the man behind the gun who counts for more than the gun, so in peace no law and no physical quality of the country can avail if there is not the right type of man to take advantage of it. The advantages count just as good weapons count.
[pb 107 425:0479] All of us who [-xxxx xxxx-] were in the lesser war know that it is a poor business to have a black powder musket when the other man has a smokeless powder musket. At the same time, take a poor creature with the best weapon [-[illegible]-] and he will be beaten by a good man with a club. You need the right type of weapon, and the difference will be decisive between two men pretty equally matched; the weapon will turn the scale; [-xxx xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx-] but more than the right type of weapon we need the right type of man. It is the same way in citizenship. Of course we need the {adavntages|advantages} of soil and climate, but we need more the type of man to take advantage of them; and the {leson|lesson} which every American should not merely learn but should have part of his fiber is the lesson that all work that is honorable co nfers honor upon those do ing it. The point is to do the work well, not as to what the work is. If the man is a miner or ranchman, employer or employe[+e,+] business man or wageworker, lawyer, banker, mechanic, it makes no difference whatsoever, if he does what his hands find to do well and truly[+.+] [-i-][+I+]f he does not do it[+,+] then whatever the work [-xx-] is he is a poor citizen. If he does do it and does it well[+,+] then whatever the work is he is a good citizen.

There are two attributes of which as a people we need to beware more than of any others: the arrogance which looks down on those not so well off and treats them with brutal and selfish disregard for their interests, and the equally base spirit of envy, hatred, and rancor for those that are better off. Either spirit is utterly un-American, utterly alien to our institutions, utterly alien to the spirit shown alike by the men who founded this republic and the men who under the lead of Abraham Linco ln save[+d+] and perpetuated the republic; (Applause) the man who in the civil war established once for all in this country the principle that we are a union in fact as well in name, and that this is a governme nt not of license but of liberty under and through the law. The worst foe to American institutions , to American life, is the man who seeks to cause hatred and distrust between one body of Americans and any other body of Americans. It is just as base no matter whether the appeal is made to inflame section against section, creed against creed, or class against class; in any event the appeal is unworthy of American institutions, and if listened to will hurt none so much as those who pay heed to it. Any man who endeavors to make you do wrong under the plea that it is in your interest to follow him in doing wrong will [-xx xxx xxxxxx xxxxx-] do wrong to you if the opportunity arises. The only safe principle upon which to act is
[pb 108 425:0479] in accordance with the immutable law of decency and fair dealing, to give each man a chance, to give one man the same justice that you give to any other, to draw the {lime|line} not between one class and another on artificial grounds, not between rich man and poor man, but between the man who is straight and the man who is crook d, without any reference to whether [-xx-] one be rich or one be poor. That is the only ground upon which you can afford to act. (Applause)

There is one thing that has pleased me particularly in going through this {coumtry|country}, whether I was {oh|on} the coast of the Atlantic or on the coast of the Pacific or whether I was inland. There is one subject {ih|in} reference to which I find every American responsive; and that is the subject of the United States navy. This republic of ours whether it wishes it or not has become a world power with the duties and {responsibilites|responsibilities} of a world power. Our navy is that arm of the government [+in+] whose welfare every American's honor is bound up, whether we live among the Rocky Mountain[+s+] in the Mississippi valley, or on the coast of either ocean. A victory for the navy means honor to all , and a defeat to the navy would mean shame and humiliation to every American worthy to be called such. Victory for the navy is not [-xxxxx-] won on the day of the fight. It is won by the {yyears|years} of preparation which precede the fight. On the first of May in '98 when Dewey sailed into Manila [+Bay+] the credit for the victory attached not only to him and his sailors, but to every man who had had aught to do with {buidding|building} the vessels which went into the bay that day, with securing the right training for the men who handled t hem. In batt le the only shots that count are the shots that hit; and if the guns do not hit they might just as well not be fired. And with such delicate mechanism as that of the formidable war engines of the present time there must be training in advance or it is out of the question to get good work out of them. The navy must be built up in time of peace if it is to be efficient in time of war. [-And-] [-t-][+T+]he one man who se work in the government rises so complete[-[illegible]-][+l+]y above all questions of partisanship that it affects alike all Americans jealous of the honor and reputation of [-xxx xxxxxxx-] their country is the Secretary of the Navy, and I now introduce to you Mr. Moody, the Secretary of the Navy of the United States. (Cheers and applause)
