My friends and fellow-citizens:

It is with a peculiar feeling of pleasure that I come to the state of Oregon. It has never before been my good fortune to visit it; and yet I know your people, I feel like them and I believe in them. I know what you have done; I know the standard of conduct you have set, and it is the standard [-whichx-] to[+ward+] which I believe all our people should strive. In greeting all of you, I wish to say a special word to those whose greeting ever touches me more than that of any other men, to the men of the Grand Army, to the men who in the Civil War dared and did [?things;?] because in big times they did things as that placard over there says, it was because [-[illegible]-][+[?&?] what they did+] that we have a country now, that we have [-got-] a President that can come to the Pacific slope and be in his own country .(Applause.) I also want to say a word of greeting to my own comrades, the men, some of whom were in the little war in '98. On behalf of them I [-wish to-] say that we wished at least to show that we had the spirit in us which you had a right to expect that the generation coming after you would have. We had in our war a trouble that did not touch the big war. In our case there was not enough war to go around. It was a little task that was set us, relatively speaking, and we did it, and now as a consequence of doing it this country's future has opened up on the Pacific; and it has become evident that whether we will or no, we must play as a nation a great part in the world. It is not open to us , my countrymen, to decide whether or not we will play it; all that we can decide is whether we will play it well or ill; and such being the case I can guarantee what the answer of America will be. (Applause.)

When I come here to this state, founded in the early forties by the men who came across the plains with empire in their [-[illegible]-][+hands+], while I speak to you I do not have to say that I preach not the life of ease, but the life of effort; not the life of those who seek only safety but the life of those who exult in daring if the time calls for daring, who flinch from no risk, from no {efgort|effort}, if only the effort
[pb 41 425:0446] and the risk are to be justified by the prize at the end. When in '61 Abraham Lincoln called you to battle there were men of little faith who said that the days of greatness of the country were ended, that you could not again make the flag whole, but you and those like you did just what they said was impossible to do; and you did it because in life there were things you prized more than life {itselg|itself}, because you had in you the capacity to feel the lift that generous souls feel when called upon in the name of an ideal. This country has risen because it has had within it the men who dared and did, the men who dared to be great, the men to whom an obstacle was something not to be shirked, but to be conquered; and I believe we shall go forward to an ever increasing greatness in the future, because I do not believe that the sons of such sires will prove false to the memories left them by their sires .

In citizenship I ask for the qualities shown [-k-] not only by the men of the civil[-x-] war, but by the men who founded this state , by the pioneers, the men who came across desert and mountain paths, or afterwards [-xx-] up the seacoast, the men who in this new world as Lowell said"pitched new states as old world men pitched tents". [-[illegible]-] [-[?those men could they have won,?]-] Would we be standing here to-day if they had possessed merely the {gehtler|gentler}, milder, softer virtues? No! I believe in the gentler, milder, softer virtues, and I believe in having others as well. I believe of course in the man being decent, cleanly in life and deed and thought, acting squarely by his neighbors, being a good husband, a good father, a decent man to deal with and careful to do his duty by his fellows and by the state and the nation. Of course I believe in that first. {?If|If} a man has not got the fundamental base of decency in him, then the abler he is the worse he is; just as in your days the abler a man was if he were a traitor, the more dangerous he was. But in addition to that quality we need others. I do not care how patriotic a man was in '61, if he ran away you could do nothing with him.
