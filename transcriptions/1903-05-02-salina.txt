Mr. Chairman; Senator; and you, My Fellow Citizens:

I am glad to be here. I am glad to address this audience with the children in the foreground and the representatives of the colleges right behind them. I have been impressed more and more as I have gone through Kansas with the wisdom of your conduct in steadily developing the highest type of intellectual and moral citizenship, and with the abounding well-being of your community. I want to say how glad I am to see the representatives of the colleges of Salina and the representatives of Bethany. I know all about Bethany. I know its work right through.

I have been struck in coming through this state as in coming through Nebraska to the north of it with the change in the scenery made by the hand of man, the effect in beautifying landscape no less than in adding to the liv[-[?e?]-]ableness[?,?] and productivity of the state, because of what has been done by the farmers who have built up this state . I rejoice that so great a state is to produce the schol[-[?e?]-][+a+]r, the man with trained mind, exactly as it produces the man with trained muscles. [-xxx-] Here as I see the children I want to congratulate you for another reason. I believe in your stock and I am glad it is not likely to die out. (Applause) I congratulate you {o n|on} the quality and quantity, both. (Applause)

The educational institutions that I am addressing, St. John's, Wesleyan, Bethany, all of them, are paying heed --to their honor be it said -- that band of devoted men and women who take charge of the public schools are paying heed, not only to the development of the body and the mind, but to the development of what counts for so much more than either body or mind -- the development of character. (Applause) That is what counts in the long run -- character. Let any one of you {l ook|look} at his neighbors. Who is successful? The man of intellect? Yes, if he has got something more than intellect. But it {mattersnot|matters not} how bright he is, how brilliant he is, if he has not got the staying quality in him, if he has not got the courage, perseverence, dogged resolution not to accept defeat, then his intellect will not bring him success.

[pb 36 425:0342]

The man that wins is the man that has in him the quality[-xx-] to which we allude when we say of a man that he is not only a good man, but a man.

As I came here I heard some cheering which made me feel a little as if I [-xxx xxx-] were at a football game. A little distance back I was presented with a football by a representative of one of the local teams, and I said to them there that the best advice I could give to {use|us} in the great game of life is the advice upon which a good football player acts -- don't flinch, don't foul, and hit the line hard. (Applause) That counts in life just as it does in sports. Do not flinch, do not be afraid, do not wait to have an easy time and sit at home and wonder why the world does not appreciate you. You are not going to win out that way. Go out and do your work. In the next place do it squarely; do [-[illegible]-][+not+] foul, do not wrong anyone; live so that the man with whom you are brought into contact will feel that while you are able to take care of yourself, (and if you are not no one will take care of you) you do not wrong any one else. Then hit the line hard; go at it; try to do your life work on the assumption that it is worth doing, and if you do you will succeed.

In closing, just one word [-xx-] of thanks and gratitude to the men of the Grand[-xx xxxx-], Army . Everywhere I go in Kansas I see the men to whom we owe it that there is a chance for me to be here at all; that there is any country for us to celebrate; that there is any President to come to any part of this country. That is so. {Wehave|We have} gotten so accustomed to the fact that we often forget its significance. If we were now split up into a jangling knot of contemptible little commonwealths (and that is what we would be if it were not for you)there would not be one of us holding up his head as a proud citizen of the nation which is not only one of the foremost in the {worl|world} now, but [-xx-] a nation with an absolutely limitless future of greatness, of service, and of improvement during the centuries to come. And I am happy to say to you children, to you students of an older growth, that I do not have to ask you to pay heed to my preaching. I ask you to pay heed to the practice of the men whom you see around you in this community, the men who from '61 to '65 rendered the supreme service to the nation when Abraham Lincoln called for that service. (Applause) Of course I believe in you; of course I am certain of your future, and I am certain of the future of all of us because I think the American nation as a whole is composed of much such material as that I am addressing to-day.

(Great applause)
