Mr. Chairman; and you, my fellow citizens, men and women of Indiana:

I am glad to have the chance of meeting you this morning. I have enjoyed greatly my ride through your beautiful state; I have enjoyed pa[+s+]sing through your rich country districts and through city after city growing up to be an industrial center. Right in this region you have many resources. You have natural gas; you have capacities within and upon the soil. The resources would amount to nothing were it not for the fact that you have the men able to develop them. Nothing would illustrate more the changing social condition of our country, the way in which we are being bound one to another in closer intimacy, than the change which is going on here and there throughout our land, and I understand is taking place right here in the methods of mail delivery. I understand that on October 1st you are to have free delivery for the city, and that by the middle of the month you expect to have complete mail service for the entire county. Just think of what a dream that would have seemed to the men who first settled here in Indiana! Think of what it would have meant to those men a century, no, not even that-a half century ago, if [-xx-] they had been told such a thing was possible! The railroads, the telegraph, the telephone, have absolutely revolutionized our social life and our whole industrial life. In consequence we have seen the growth of the great industrial organizations that come as inevitably as rural free delivery itself in consequence of the change in conditions. It is thus aggregations of capital can be employed to such an advantage. They will inevitably be so employed. The effort to destroy them would be futile in the first place, and if it were not futile it would be mischievous in the second. We do not wish to try to destroy the great industrial aggregation[+s+]. What we wish to do is to make [-it-][+them+] subserve the public good. When any new tendency comes in the business world or the social world[+,+] at first there is [pb 66 424:0751] dislocation; there are troubles and difficulties; there comes evil with the good. Those people are short sighted and unwise who would seek to destroy the evil by a method which would at the same time do away with the good. We need to show not merely resolution, but sanity, intelligence, and common sense in dealing with the great problems caused by the presence of these industrial combinations. We do not war on them; we war on any evil in them. And you can reach that evil[+,+] I am certain[+,+] only through exercising national control over them. In the old days each state could deal with the corporations within its borders; but the corporations were small; their field of activity limited. Now the {extraoddinary|extraordinary} revolution wrought by the change in the methods of shipment of merchandise and of transmission of news has brought about a condition which in [-M-] my judgment renders it inevitable that the nation should assume the power of supervising and regulating these great industrial combinations. Not for one moment in a spirit of hostility to those that do well, but with the firm determination that they shall do well; with a firm determination that every individual, public or private, [-individual-][+single+] or corporate shall obey the will of the people as expressed in the fundamental law of the land.

It is pleasant to come here[+,+] to see the evidences of material well-being round about me. In thanking all of those who acted as my escort when I came from the car to the platform let me say a particular word for those whom I saw at the other end of the line-the men who had fought in the Philippines, or in the Spanish War, a very small war[+ comrades,+] compared to the war you saw from '61 to '65; but the boys had the same spirit in them after all. (Cries of "That is right!" and applause.) There was not a chance to do much [-[illegible]-] except to show that they had in them the wish to do what their fathers did. Now I was struck as I came up here by seeing that one of the first men [+was a man+] in a sailor[+'+]s uniform . He had been with Dewey at Manila. Just as I said before in speaking of the great change wrought [-xx-] by closer interchange of communication, so it is true as [pb 67 424:0752] another consequence[-x-] of that change that we are knit together closer for good or for ill than ever before in the nation's history. It was from the inland state of Vermont that {Deway|Dewey} came, and here from the still further inland state of Indiana I come upon some of Dewey's men. It makes no difference to us where the man came from if he wore the uniform honorably and well, be he northerner or southerner or [?easterner?] or westerner. If his metal rang true in the times that tried men's souls we hail him as brother and comrade; we are proud of him as a fellow American. (Applause)

You have done much here in developing the material resources of this country {bewause|because} you had in you the stuff that made you able to [-[?take?]-] develop them. A good deal can be done by combination among ourselves; something can be done through the governmental agencies o[+f+] the state and the nation, but oh,my friends and fellow countrymen, let no one ever delude you into the belief that any law or any association or combination can take the place of the man's own individual qualities as the determining factor in working out his success! No tactics at the time of the civil war, no organization woul[+d+] make a good soldier out of a man who did not have [-xx-] the stuff in him. When you got down to the time of emergencies[+,+] [-[?what?]xxxx-] when each man was left on his own resources, then what you wanted to know of the man on your right hand or your left, was whether he would"stay put". That was it, wasn't it? Exactly! And so now to make a good citizen[+,+] laws will help, constitution will help. We can easily enough spoil all his chances by bad laws. Heaven knows that is easy enough! We can by good laws give him the best opportunity by which to work out his own salvation[?,?] but he has to take advantage of the opportunity[-xx-] himself. No one can supply in him the lack of the capacity thus to take advantage of it.

Every man [-[illegible]-] stumbles. There is not a man here who does not at some time or other need a helping hand to be stretched out toward him; and shame to the man who when the need arises would not thus stretch out a helping hand to his brother. But if the man lies down you cannot carry him. You can help him up if he stumbles, but if [pb 68 424:0752] wants to be carried he is not worth carrying. Isn't that true? Exactly! You will often hear people talk as if some how the government could do something more and make us all prosperous. Now[+,+] the government is us here, you and I; the rest of us right around here, and you can tell about [-h[illegible]-] what the government can do on a large scale by seeing what it can do on a small scale in this city, in this county. It can do a good deal. You can do more for it than it can do for you everytime. [-Itxisxthexmanxthatx-] Certain things can be done for our people through national legislation. I urge that those things be done. But in the last resort when everything has been said and done it remains true that this country is going to succeed in the future for the reason because of which it has succeeded in the past-[-[illegible]-] the high average of citizenship of the ordinary American citizen. (Applause)
