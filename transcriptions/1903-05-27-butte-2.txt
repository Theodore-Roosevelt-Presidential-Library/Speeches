[pb 139 425:0495]

Mr. Gillis;  and you, Gentlemen and Ladies:

I thank you heartily for this unexpected gift. I am deeply pleased at receiving it; and I accept it as one who tries to be an American President, acting upon the principle of giving a square deal to each and every man. (Cheers and applause)

[note: In the typed heading, "accepting" reads "accppting" and "presented" has a p struck over its second e on the typewriter; "Roosevelt" is typed with a gap ("Roo sevelt"). The title line gives the intended words.]
[note: The second listed page (right half of frame 0495) is not typescript: it holds a handwritten note explaining that at the conclusion of the President's address at Columbia Gardens, Butte, a souvenir was presented on behalf of the Silver Bow Trades and Labor Assembly by Malcolm Gillis, with a pasted newspaper clipping of Gillis's presentation and the reply. Not transcribed here.]
