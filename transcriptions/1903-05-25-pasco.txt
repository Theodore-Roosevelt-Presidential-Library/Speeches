My Fellow Citizens:

I believe absolutely that in [+the+] irrigation of this region lies the golden promise [-haxx-] hardly to be exceeded by [-that-][+the+] opening in Puget Sound itself. No more important law has been put upon the stat[-e-][+[?ute?]+] books of recent years than the national irrigation act; and with the policy thus opened, thus begun, [-xxxx-] with the help of the state and the nation joined to the activity of individuals by themselves or in associations , I believe that[+,+] in this locality [+for instance+] the waters of the Columbia and the Snake[+,+] can be used so as to make[+,+] as your chairman said, a veritable Garden of Eden of all this region. (Applause) {Whemethe|When the} water can be put on we all of us know that rain is a poor substitute for irrigation. As I have passed through this country to-day I have been struck by the extraordinary fertility of the fields and orchards thus brought under irrigation, of their yield in fruit, of their yield in sugar, in alfalfa, of their yield in all things, [-xxxx-]when once the water can be put upon the land. You need to have the water distributed in space and collected in time. You need to have the irrigation ditches and the reservoirs-- reservoirs to keep the water that runs to waste in one season so that it can be used in the season when it is needed. Many of those enterprises are so large that private individuals cannot undertake them. There must be aid through the nation and the state; aid in the way of positively doing the work; aid in making the experime nt to see how the work can be done. I think that but few of our people outside of the regions immediately affected fully realize the far-reaching importance of the policy entered on by the national government last year in regard to irrigation; a policy which has and must have steadily as its object the creation of homes on the soil and the use of the irrigable lands for actual allotment to the people who are to live upon them and to bring [-xxx-] up their children upon them. We must be most careful that they do not get in large tracts [-xx xx-] into the hands of speculators. The prime object must be to preserve them for actual use by those who take advantage of them. The nation and the state can help of course , but fundamentally it remains true and must remain true here as everywhere else that all that can be done by nation and state is to aid in creating conditions which will enable each man to take advantage of his own thrift, energy, industry and foresight. Nothing can take the place of tho se qualities. [-xxx xxxxx-] What has been done in this great state is due not merely to the state's natural advantages, indispensable though they are, but to the quality of citizenship of her people, of her men and women. I cannot too often repeat that the state goes up or down accordi ng to the type of the average citizen in the state. (Cheers and applause)
