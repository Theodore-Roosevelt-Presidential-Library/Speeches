My friends and fellow citizens: I am glad indeed to have the chance of greeting you to-day, and I know that even the men of the Grand Army who fought in the big war wont object to my saying that I am particularly pleased to be greeted by my comrades of the small war (Applause). The {fightin|fight in} which we were was a very small fight indeed compared with the war in which you struggled, but we hope that we showed ourselves not entirely unworthy of you. It is of course a particular pleasure to be here with the members of one company of the regiment [-in-][+beside+] [+whom+] [-[?whose company?]-] I served at Santiago. I want to draw just one lesson from our experiences. You who followed Grant and Sherman and Thomas and Sheridan - you had different arms from those carried by the troops who followed Washington, you were drilled with different tactics; but the spirit that [-dxxxxx-] drove you to victory was the same as the spirit that wrought the victory of Yorktown. And so [-[illegible]-] the men who go down to battle at the present day have different weapons, are drilled in open order instead of in the elbow touch of the old time; but to win they have to have the same spirit that you had. We need good weapons. I want to see the day when every national guardsman will have a smokeless powder high power rifle; and it always irritates me to see a man in the United States uniform carrying a weapon which in actual battle is about as efficient as a crossbow (Laughter). The best is none too good for any man who [-x-] has the uniform that Uncle Sam's troops wear (Applause). [-[?Wexhaxxhadxexperiex?]-] We have experienced what it meant to have black powder weapons. But after you have got the best weapon, you have to have the right caliber of man behind it (Applause) Now it is just so in civil life. We can devise constitutions, we can devise legislation, and it is highly necessary to have them. As I say, the best man with poor weapons cannot begin to do what he could with good weapons. We need good laws, we need honest and fearless administration of the law; but exactly as it is the man behind the gun that counts most - more than the gun even - so in civil life it is the standard of citizenship in the

[pb ? 424:0610]

community that is the all-important factor in producing good government. There are other republics, some modeled on ours, with constitutions and laws akin to ours, in which nevertheless the experiment of free government if not a failure has at least come lamentably short of what we would have a right to hope. And the reason is that while the forms were the same the men were different. Do not forget that. In the last resort it is the high average of the individual citizen upon which we must depend for success in our great experiment of showing[-[illegible]-]that a republic can not only be free but be the mightiest nation on the face of the earth (Applause). There is not any patent device for getting good citizenship; there is not any patent device for getting good government. If you meet any man who tells you that he has one grand, simple specific which will remedy all the ills of the body politic, he is a quack (LAughter and applause). We will in the future get good government, good citizenship, just as you got good government and good citizenship in the days of Lincoln - just as you got them in the days of Washington. There will be new methods, but there has to be the same spirit behind the methods, or we will fail. It is not going to be simple to get it either. It was not simple to put down the Rebellion - not a bit. There were people who said it was to be a 60 days war. There were excellent people who stayed at home, who said that you ought to go into Richmond at once. They said it was very discreditable to Lincoln that you did not go into Richmond at once. They weren't near Richmond themselves (LAughter). There were obstacles there. And it took you four years after the firing on Fort Sumpter before Appamattox came. Who are the men whom we [-[?x?]-] honor now? The men with patent devices to end everything at the beginning of the war? the men of little faith, who after Bull Run said the war was over? You said it was not. It took three years and nine months before you made your words good - but you made them good. And then the war was over, and it was over the other way (Laughter and applause). You won not by trying to do the whole business out of hand in 30 or 60 days - you won by realizing the magnitude of the task, realizing that it was not going to be solved in just one way - that it was going to be solved by the steady, continuous effort of men who refused to be daunted by failure, who re-

[pb ? 424:0611]

fused to give up hope because there was no immediate royal road to victory, who faced defeat and came up again, and if they had to meet defeat again came up again until out of defeat they wrested the splendid ultimate triumph. That is how you won in the civil war (Applause). And that is how we are going to win in civil life in overcoming any of the evils that exist in the body politic. It is awfully easy, and to some souls attractive, to have hysterics about evils. [-[?They?]xxxxxxxxx-] You do not conquer them that way. You conquer them by facing them, by making up your mind that if you do not know about them they may not be as bad as when you do know [?about?] them . That is another little fact that is worth keeping in mind.And then starting in to overcome them by the exercise of the old, old qualities of citizenship, just as you started in in the civil war to overcome your foes by the exercise of the same qualities that have made good soldiers from the days of Hannibal and Alexander and Caesar down. You needed courage, honesty and intelligence, fertility of resource. And so to bring this nation to the governmental standard which we have a right to expect that it shall attain we need to display the qualities that have made good citizens ever since the days {df|of} the republics of antiquity - intelligence, resolution, the desire for fair and square dealing with one's neighbors; and among them all no quality do we need more than the saving grace of common sense. Great applause).
