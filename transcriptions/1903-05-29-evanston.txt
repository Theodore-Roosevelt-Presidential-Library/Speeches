Senator Clark; and you, My Fellow Citizens; My Fellow Americans; Men and Women of Wyoming:

When I come to Wyoming I feel that I have gotten back to a state I know well. It was twenty years ago that I first came within your borders. It was at the other end of the state, in the north[-west-][+east+] corner, at the Powder and the head waters of the Belle Fourche and the Clear Creek, in the days when I was a cow man myself. The Senator has said that I am blind to your faults. Well, when I discover them I will be blind to them. Friends, I have known you ; I know what you are; I believe in you[-[?,?]-] with all my heart and soul. I do not see how anyone could help believing in you who has had the good fortune to know you as I know you. I have worked with you, and played with you, and occasionally fought with you. I know you pretty well. I was struck [-by-] at a little bit of information that came to me the other day. Wyoming sent relatively to her population a very large [-number of-] percentage of men into the Spanish war; and of those she sent in -- one thousand or over in number -- there was not one man who was illiterate. Not a single one of W[-[illegible]-]yoming's volunteers was illiterate; and my belief in the [-xxxxx-] future of the state is based in no small degree upon the way in which you are taking care that the next generation, like the present, shall be trained in mind as well as in body, and in what counts for more than mind or body -- in character. Wyoming is a typical western state. There will be much development in time to come within her borders; and one of the most efficient sources of that {developemnt|development} will be irrigation; not only in the way of irrigated agriculture, but in the way of {helpin g|helping} the ranchman by providing through {iri gation|irrigation} winter feed immediately adjoining the summer range.

Nothing has pleased me more as President than the chance given me to affix my name to the act creating for the first time a national interest in and supervision over irrigation. And I wish here to state that among all those who helped me in Congress to bring about the passage of that law there were no men who helped me [?more?] than Senators Clark and Warren and Congressman Mondell. [-[illegible]-] (Applause) It was a little difficult to make some of our excellent [-[?people?]-] friends understand that if it was right for the nation to take care of the navigable water courses (and some that were only presumptive[+ly+] navigable ) along the lower portions of the rivers, it was
[pb 173 425:0513] even more right for the nation to take care of storing and distributing the water in the upper portions of those water courses in the arid regions. The government can do much for Wyoming in connection with this irrigation work, because there are certain great works, notably storage reservoirs, the construction of which should be undertaken rather by the government than by private individuals or associations of individuals. The state itself can do something; and, finally, most of all [-xxx-] must be done by the people themselves, by the men acting individually or in association. The nation can help them, and must help them, and will help them; but fundamentally in irrigation as in everything else all that the nation, all that the state can do is to aid the man who can help himself. (Applause) In the long run that is the only help that can be given.

You here whom I am addressing, you and those who went before you, have built this state up on the plains and among the mountains, because each of you had in him the capacity for such self-help. Nothing can take the place of the individual man's power of self-reliance, self-restraint, self-mastery, self-control, his power of forcing his soul upward and upward, and recognizing in an obstacle something not to be flinched from, but to be overcome, to be trampled under foot. Good laws are good things. Bad laws are very bad things. Upright, clean, fearless administration of the law is a better thing; but fundamentally what determines the success of any state is [+the+] character of the average man, the average woman, in that state. If we have the right type of man and woman the state will go {pon|on} and succeed. If we have not laws are wasted, natural resources are wasted. California had been for seventy-five years in the possession of an alien people when our people {f irst|first} went within its borders and it had not advanced at all. We took it, and in ten years had made it one of the leading commonwealths of the land. We have a good constitution and good laws. There are other governments that have practically exactly our constitution, exactly our laws; and they have failed where we have succeeded because they did not have the right type of man behind the laws. (Applause)

It is just as it was in the civil war. You needed to have the right kind of weapon; it was a good thing to have a nice uniform; a better thing to have training; but in spite of the weapon, in spite of the uniform, in spite of the training, if you did not have the right stuff in you it could not be gotten out of you. You won because you had the fighting edge; because you had the power to win vic[pb 174 425:0513]tory; and if defeat came to set your face like flint and try it again until victory [-xxxx-].finally came. It is the same thing in civil life. Every one of us at times stumbles. Every one of us at times needs to have a helping hand stretched out to him. There is not one of us here that does not sometimes need that hand. Shame to the man who when the occasion comes will not help up his brother who has for the moment fallen. Help him up, but if he lies down you cannot carry him. Fundamentally you can help him in just one way; that is to help him help himself. That is the only way in which real help can be given.

I believe in what you are doing, {inwresting|in wresting} material success out of this land; in the way you are building up the mine and the ranch; in what is done by your railroads, by your men of law, by your men of business; that is all indispensable as the foundation. The first thing any man has got to do, that any woman has to do[-[?;?]-] is to do the duties that are nearest at hand. The man who would like to be a hero[+,+] but meanwhile will not earn his own {livi ng|living}[+,+] is of no use in the community; and the woman, if I may venture to say so, who would only pay attention to the elegance and refinements of life and not to the necessary things, is not of much use to herself. You see I [-[illegible]-][+speak+] guardedly. The prime thing that any man has to do is to pull his own weight. He must be able to earn his own {livingand|living and} to support those dependent upon him. He must be able to do the homely, everyday dut[-y-]ies which if left undone mean[-[?s?]-] destruction to the individual and destruction to the state . But after all has been done[+,+] after we have gotten the material well-being, on it we must build the superstructure of {te|the} higher life -- moral, intellectual, spiritual -- or the [+mere+] material advancement will go for but little. (Applause) I congratulate you of Wyoming upon the way in which you prepared for the building of that higher life . Not only here in Wyoming but elsewhere as I go through these western states, which I knew so well nearly a quarter of a century ago, I am more struck and more pleased than I can say by the growth in the appreciation of beauty, of all that makes life pleasanter, more beautiful and nobler. It is a delight to see the progress that has been made; and my heart swells with pride as an American as I see it, and as I realize how pace by pace with the material development {ha s|has} gone on the development based upon it and built upon it, the development t[+h+]at tells for culture, for all that culture brings with it, that tells for {produci ng|producing} the best type of man and woman.

[pb 175 425:0514]

In greeting all of you I want to say a special word of greeting to those whom I see carrying babies. I congratulate you upon all your products, but most of all upon the children. I like your stock and am glad to see it is being kept up. Of course it is a mere truism to say that the -all-important thing for the [-xxxxxxxxx-] future of this state, for the future of this {count ry|country}, is the [-kind-] kind of men and women who will make that future; and the kind of men and women will be determined largely by the kind of boys and girls you [-xxxx xxx-] train up to-day. The education must lie partly in the hands of the educators, the school teachers, the college professors, the college presidents; but fundamentally also in the hands of the father and mother, who cannot put off upon the shoulders of anyone else their duty, their burden. You cannot make the child by preaching do anything that you do not practice. It is no use [-[illegible]-][+to+] {pre ach|preach} to children to tell the truth if the father and mother occasionally lapse from it. It is no use to teach them to do their duty, if the elder folks do not do theirs. It is no use to {pr each|preach} unselfishness to a boy in a household where the father is selfish; or self-restraint to the girl in the household where the mother has none. If you will not be good citizens for your own sakes I hope you will be such for the sake of the children.

There is one subject upon which I want to touch. Wherever I have gone i[+n+] this country I have found the answer instantaneous to the appeal for what concerns all Americans -- the United States navy. As a nation we have to play a great part in the world whether we will or not. A small nation can honorably play a small part. A big nation cannot play a [-xxxxxxx-] small part. It has to play a big part. It can play that big part badly if it wishes;; but it has got to play it . I know you too well my fellow countrymen to have any question as to your determination that this country shall play a big part and shall play it well. We cannot play that part in foreign affairs unless we are able to make our word good . The poorest creature in private life is the man who blusters and then backs down. The man you respect is the man {wh o|who} does not bluster, who does not get into a fight unless he has to; but if he does, sees it through. I want to see America internationally take just that position. Speak respectfully of other nations. If there is anything cheap and contemptible it is to abuse foreign {nati ons|nations}; and
[pb 176 425:0514] especially to have any of our public men abuse foreign nations. It is always undignified, and possibly harmful. Speak {decentlyand|decently and} respectfully of them. Do not go out of your way to say unpleasant things about them; but when you have made up your mind that the interest and honor of America require [-xxxxxxx-] that a certain position be taken, take it and back it up. In order to do that we need to go on with the upbuilding of the navy, and to keep it at the highest point of efficiency; [-and now I introduce to you Mr. [?Moody?], the Secretary of the Navy-]. (Cheers and applause)
