Senator[+Gamble+][?;?] My {Fe llow|Fellow} Americans:

I am glad to have the chance of being here in South Dakota , and I feel at home. I have been here many times before. (Applause) Twenty-three years ago I came to Dakota when it was a territory, and if there are any cow punc[+h+]ers in the audience I might claim special acquaintance with them; and in greeting all of you I know that the rest will not object to my saying a special word of greeting to the men of the Grand Army. We as {citizenscan|citizens can} succeed in doing our work just in proportion as we do it in the way they did theirs from '61 to '65. I want to say another word of greeting to the best possible exhibit of South Dakota's product -- the children. [-xxh-]They seem to be all right in quality and all right in quantity. As I like the stock I am glad it is to be kept up. (Cheers and applause)

Now a word to you here in this state, whose leading industries are agriculture, and in {t e|the} west mining[+--+] a state of farmers and miners. I do not have to speak of the prosperity we have enjoyed, because you have shared in it to an unusual degree. I understand that South Dakota stands highest of all the states of the union in the aggregate productiveness of wealth per head during the year. That prosperity is due to many causes, not to any one. Mere legislation, mere governmental work, never can make it. If the rain does not fall or if it falls too much, ther[+e+] is not any way of making a law about it. If the hand of the Lord is heavy upon us, in flood or in drought, in disease or pestilence so far as these cannot be controlled, then the wit of man cannot devise a perfect remedy.

Furthermore in addition to dealing with the elements outside of the control of man, only under the Control of Providence, we must ever keep in mind that the {fundemental|fundamental} factor in any man's success is the man's own qualities[+[?,?]+] his strength, his courage, his hard[+i+]hood, his thrift, his wisdom. Here in our own experience in this state during the days of its statehood and the days when it was a territory, we have seen the effort made to grow what could not be grown in certain localities, ruin follow, the locality {woudd|would} apparently be abandoned[+,+] and then occupied again when men had the sense to take[+[?up?]+] an industry that was adapted to it. You can grow cattle where you cannot grow wheat.

[pb 62 425:0223]

We will win out primarily according to the way in which the average American does his work. I spoke to the men of the Grand Army, I can draw the lesson that I am now drawing from their experience as well as from our own in civil life. [-In the army-], In the civil war , you won. To win you needed good leaders, you needed the genius of Lincoln, you needed the backing of the people, but what you needed most was the {co urage|courage} and hardihood of the individual fighting man. You {carriedthe|carried the} banner of the {rppublic|republic} in triumph to Appomattox, because the average man had in him the stuff out of which soldiers are made.

As it was in war, so it must be in civil life. No law that ever was devised or ever can be devised will make a [-xxxxx-] coward brave, or a fool wise, or a weakling strong. Fundamentally you have to have the right stuff in you or you cannot get it out of you. (Applause) You have[+[?it?]+] here on the prairie, and [+have+] turned this into the great state that it is, because you have had that stuff in you. That was not easy; not a bit; you did not have easy times in the war,either. The things in this life that are really worth having do not usually come to those who wish to lead lives of mere ease. I am speaking to pioneers or to the sons of pioneers, and any man who does pioneering work does rough work, and if he is not fit to do it he will go to the wall in a trice. It is hard work to make a new state, a new country, it is a work fit for men to do and only men can do it, and it takes men to do {ittoo|it too}(Applause) You had men to do it, and you did it.

It is difficult to get prosperity by law, but good laws can help in getting it, and it is easy enough to mar it by poor laws. We are getting along well as a nation. We have got prosperity. We have gotten that prosperity under a certain economic scheme of finance {ad|and} tariff. Be cautious how you go into any drastic change that may upset it all. There is nothing sacred about a tariff schedule. As it becomes necessary to change a schedule, to make it fit the shifting needs of a nation, do it; [-xxx-] Remember , however, {t hat|that} there is more to be considered than merely whether one given schedule should be changed. All the schedules have got to be considered as part of the whole, and, while it is vitally important that we should not become fossilized in our economic policy, that we should be able to {r eapply|reapply} the details[+to+] the shifting needs of the nation; remember that the change must not be made until we are convinced that the need is so great as to more than offset the inevitable dislocation that will come from

[pb 63 425:0224]

it, and that when the {whange|change} is made it {shoud|should} be a change in the application of details of the scheme, and not a change in the scheme of the policy under and through which we have attained to our present pitch of prosperity.

I have but a moment. It has been a real pleasure to see you, and I can sum up all I have to say to you in just a couple of phrases. {Yoy|You} need wise laws. See that you get them. You need honest administration of the laws. See that you have it. But do not make the mistake of thinking that any law, or any administration of the law, can take the place of the fundamental qualities that make a good individual citizen and make a good nation[+--+] the qualities of honesty, of courage, and of common sense.
