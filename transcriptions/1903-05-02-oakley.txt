My friends and fellow-citizens:

It is a great pleasure to see you. I am glad to see the men, the women and the children. I have enjoyed to the full traveling to-day through Kansas. [+It has been a great pleasure [-[illegible] [?through?] [illegible]-] to see+] [-T-][+t+]he extraordinary changes made by the hand of man in this country . I never lived in Kansas. I have lived farther north, in the Dakotas,in much this type of country, and it is astounding to {mee|see} the changes that have been made . Much of it I cannot understand; much of it has been done since my day. I do not know quite what crops you are raising. I have seen enough, however to appreciate to the full all the progress that it means. There is one thing, ladies and gentlemen, with which I am particularly pleased and that is the {signsthat|signs that} the intellectual and spiritual progress of your people are not being neglected, that they go on hand in hand with the material progress. It is a great pleasure to see the school houses, to see in many of the towns through which I have passed, the libraries, to see the efforts made for mental betterment; and while it is a good thing to raise the crops that you have raised, the cattle and grain, it is a better thing to produce the high type of men and women. It is the citizenship of the state that really counts, and I have abounding confidence in your future,because I have abounding confidence in you. I believe in you, in what you have done, and what you can do; in your capacity for self-help and your capacity to work one with the other. I think that here you are developing the qualities that tell for decency and honesty, and the qualities that tell for strength and hard[+i+]hood..(Cheers and applause.)
