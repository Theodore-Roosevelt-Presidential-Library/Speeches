My Friends and Fellow [-xxxxxxxx-] Countrymen:

It is a peculiar pleasure to me to stop here because {interestedthough|interested though} I have been [-xx-] traveling throughout this great country in its material development, in that material well-being which must be the foundation of national greatness, I am even more interested in the superstructure built upon that foundation, the superstructure which is the national greatness, the training for the higher life, moral, intellectual and spiritual, which must come through such institutions of learning as that in this city. [-x-] Therefore I shall ask that this once the veterans of the great war whom I always greet first allow me[-xxxxxxx-]instead to greet the next generation, our {m asters|masters} of the {fu ture|future}, the young men and young women who {arestudents|are students} to-day and upon [+whom+] the whole welfare of the nation and the state will depend to-morrow. (Applause) I congratulate our country in the way in which we {seduously|sedulously} care for {theeducation|the education} of these masters of the future. It is a mere truism to say that what we wish to do in this country is not merely to develop it for [-for-] our own use, but while using it to leave it as a better heritage {thanwe|than we} received, a better heritage to the generation that is to come after us. Everyone of you knows in dealing with his own private life that [+it+] is no use to leave a fortune to a child who is certain to waste it, who is certain to misuse it. The fortune is a curse and not a blessing in a case like that. So with our nation[+;+] [+T+]his great nation[+,+] made what it is by the valor of the men who fought in the civil war; made what it is by the craft of statesman, of soldier, of captain of industry, of the architect of our industrial welfare; by our educators, our writers, by all[-x-] the people who in the past have made the name of America[-n-] great, must of course depend for its future upon the kind of men and women which the next generation produces, that is upon the kind of leaders among the men and women of the next generation, which the students of to-day show themselves to be. Now a word to you students -- much has been given to you and much will rightly be expected from you.[-xxx-] Your alma mater , school, college, university, has given you the training; you cannot accept it as a gift which you do not return or else you are not true Americans. You must make a return and there is but one return you can make --the return of leadership in the art and practice of good citizenship hereafter. That is
[pb 15 425:0531] the way you can reflect credit upon your alma mater. That is the way you can show that it has been no waste to give you this training; that on the contrary it has been an investment by the state which will pay a thousand fold. The special privileges you have received confer no further special privileges hereafter. They confer [+on+] you added obligations to the state, added obligations to whatever is best in civic and social life. We have a right to expect that among you will be developed [+those+] who will take the lead in solving the many great problems which this nation has before it. It is inevitable that in the career of a great nation such great problems should arise. The path of greatness is not an easy one for the nation, not an easy one for the individual; the path of success is not an easy one. Every man here, every woman here, who has been in his or her walk of life successful[+,+] has achieved success only through effort, through self-denial, self-mastery, self-restraint, through exhibition of will, of courage, of the spirit that dares, that does, that resolves. You have got to show that in leading the nation, for the nation needs all those qualities in her sons and daughters. (Cheers and applause)
