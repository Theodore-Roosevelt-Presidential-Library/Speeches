Mr. Mayor; and you, My Fellow Citizens; Men and Women of Dwight:

Naturally I am pl[+e+]ased by the kind words the Mayor has said to me. Perhaps I prize them especially, Mr. Mayor, coming from one who is not of my party; but the whole thing is, my friends, if we are all good Americans that is enough platform for all of us to stand on. I prize more than I can say such words as have been uttered by the Mayor; and I assure you I shall do my best to try to deserve them.

I thank you for coming out to greet me to-day. I have enjoyed to the full my trip through this great state. I congratulate you and all our people upon the material well-being that we enjoy. It is an absolutely essential thing that we should have well-being; but I congratulate you even more because you of Illinois -- I think I can say that we of the nation -- strive steadily to build upon that foundation a structure of the higher life, moral, intellectual, and spiritual. The things have got to go hand in hand, just exactly as the average citizen's first business is to pull his own weight, to earn his own livelihood and the livelihood of those dependent upon him; but it is not enough. He has got in addition to that to try to develop himself and to develop those dependent upon him to have [-x-][+the+] higher attributes of good citizenship, the kind of citizenship that made this government prompt to respond to the call of Abraham Lincoln. Fundamentally, though the laws are important, and the administration of the laws is important, what is most important of all is to have the average man and the average woman the right kind of man and woman, to have them good in the home, good in the intimate relations of the household, good as husband, as father, as mother, [-[?as children,?]-] good as neighbors, good to work with and deal with, good in their relations to the state.

In greeting all of you I want to greet especially those whom I see in the crowd, men and women, carrying children. I know that in greeting all of you, you will not object to my expressing a special sense of kinship with those whom I have mentioned. (Cheers and applause)

[note: "e" is inserted by hand, with a caret, in "pl[+e+]ased". "the" is typed above the line over an x-ed out letter (apparently "a") before "higher". The words after "as mother," are struck through by hand. Hand strokes run through the second "l" of the second "livelihood" and the "t" of "to" after "him"; they do not change the words. Overtyped letters read as their final letter (if, even, that).]
