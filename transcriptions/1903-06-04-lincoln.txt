My Fellow Citizens:

It is a great pleasure to have the chance of coming [-xxxx-] before you and saying a word of greeting to you today. I am now closing a trip that has lasted over two months. I have gone across the continent from one ocean to the other and am now well on my return; and of all things on that trip, the thing that has impressed me most is the essential unity of our people.Wherever I have been, o[+n+] the Atlantic seabord, in this great Mississippi valley, on the high plains and among the mountains to the westward, and farther westward still by the shores of the pacific, [+the+] people whom I have seen are substantially one; the people whom I have seen are Americans, and nothing else, responsive to the same appeals, endeavoring to shape their lives in accordance with the same ideals. I do not think I needed to be taught the lesson of what we owe to the men who like you, my comrade there, [-to the men who-] brought to a triumphant conclusion the struggle of the great civil war; but if I did need to be taught that lesson it would have been taught by this trip, for now owing to what those men did, an American President can go from one ocean to the other, from the Gulf to the {Canafian|Canadian} border, and everywhere be at home among his fellow Americans. Those men , the men who fought in the great civil war, fought for national unity and for true liberty; a liberty that is as far asunder as the poles are from one another from license; [-xxx-][+a+] liberty that comes under, by and through the law; a union of localities, of all good citizens, who are content to accept {g od|good} [?citizenship?] as in itself the all-sufficient test of any man's character and standing. These men who fought in the civil war fought to do away once for all with the spirit of sectional hatred, once for all to dispose of that {tyoe|type} of public man who would seek to find his own profit in ministering to the sectional prejudices of his followers.

In the same way we must see to it that no other form of agitation to excite one body of Americans against another body of Americans is ever allowed to flourish in our country. (Applause) {Itis|It is} [?{abase|a base}?] and a wicked thing to try to make one American feel that another American is his foe because he lives in a different section of the country. It is just as base, just as wicked to try to make that American feel that another American is his foe because he carries on a [pb 51 425:0549] different occupation, because [-xx-]his wealth is either greater or less, or because he professes a different creed. There is no room in our system of government for any division along lines of section, creed, or class; (Applause) and the man is no true disciple of those [+who+] with Washington founded this government, who with Lincoln preserved it, if he seeks to divide our people along those lines. There are two sides to that. It shows a mean spirit, a base spirit, for one man to look down upon another because he is less well off. It is just as mean, just as base, to regard with envy, hatred and rancor another because he is better off. Either attitude is fundamentally like the other, and one is fundamentally as base as the other. The man who looks down on with arrogant contempt the one who is less well off than he is, if circumstances were reversed would with envy and malice regard the man who is better off; and in turn, the man who would seek for his personal ends, or for any other reason, to raise up strife by exciting feeling against those who happen to be better off, lacks [?but?] the opportunity to show himself the same kind of tyrant that he denounces. I cannot impress that too strongly upon you. They are [-xxxxxxxx-] opposite sides of the same crystal and a very black crystal at that. Envy and mali[-[?s?]-][+[?c?]+]e toward people who are better off are just one side of the same feeling that makes arr[-[?r?]-]ogance to[-x-]wards those who are [-xxxxxx-][+less well+] off. The decent man, the square man, is incapable of feeling such [-xxxx-] mean envy , such mean arrogance. The decent man is too self-respecting for one moment to pay the unwarranted tribute to mere wealth by envying it. He is too self-respecting to soil himself by looking down on his fellows who happen to be less well off, if they are doing straight and decent work in the world. Our people must steadfastly keep in view the ideal of conduct in greatness with which this nation has been managed in the past and shall be managed in thefuture; (Applause) steadily keep in view the ideal of a government where men are not only equal under the law; but where every man is judged not by the accidentsof position or wealth, but by the essential rule of conduct; where if a man behaves himself, if he does work worth doing and does it well, then be he farmer or townsman, be he wageworker or employer, lawyer, mechanic, business man, if he is an honest man he is treated with respect by his [pb 52 425:0549] fellows, and given the only thing that an American has a right to ask -- a square deal. (Applause) Every American is entitled to that; and if he wants more he is not entitled to anything.

It has been a great pleasure to me, coming through your state and seeing your abounding material prosperity, to realise more and more as I see school, college, university, how upon that prosperity you are building the higher life, t[-x-] [-the life,-] the life intellectual, moral, spiritual. We must, of course, have the prosperity as a basis. I do not think that there is much to be [-xxxx-] gotten out of the individual who has lofty aspirations, but will not work. (Laughter)

His first business should be to pull his own weight, to earn his own livelihood and take care of those dependent upon him, take care of his wife and children; do that first, have the aspirations afterwards; but it is a good thing to have the aspirations. It is [?not?] only a good thing, it is [-xx-] absolutely necessary [-[?thing?]-] that in addition to material well-being we as a people should build upon [-xxxx-] it the higher structure.

[-In the presence-] Here, in Illinois, the state of Abraham Lincoln which sent its sons by the hundred thousand when Lincoln [?called?], I do not have to say that of course no community is worth anything if it has not more than the material side to it; if it does not have other aspirations than those connected [-xxxh-] merely with physical well-being. Unfortunately there are people who never can be made to understand that you need two sets of attributes, and they are prone to misunderstand you when you ask for one set and think you only want that set. You must have first as a basis for success of the individual, for the success of the nation, the power to dowell in material things. That we must have first; we must have it as the basis, the foundation, upon which the superstructure must be built. If there is not in the nation the qualities of industry, thrift, business energy, business enterprise, of course you can buildnothing; for there is no foundation upon which to build. The {fundation|foundation} is the first thing, and it is indispensable, but it is not the building . Upon it you must build the higher qualities, the qualities which [+sent+] Illinois' sons to the war when Lincoln called, the qualities which made the men of '61 to '65 feel not only that material prosperity but that life itself counted as nothing compared to other things.

There is no need now for the self-devotion and self-sacrifice shown i[illegible] in those days; but there is needfor {desplaying|displaying} the same spirit displayed in '61 by the men in the blue uniform; there is need for displaying that same spirit in our civic and social lifeof to-day. And I believe in you; I believe in the {cuntry|country} of which you are part; because I believe that the average American citizen has in him just that spirit; that he has the combination of capacity to seek after the lofty ideal[-s-] with capacity to realize it in practical way. I greet you all. I want to say a speci[illegible] word of greeting to the fathers and mothers who have come down here carrying small children. I believe in children; and as I like your stock I am gladit is being kept up. (Cheers and applause)
