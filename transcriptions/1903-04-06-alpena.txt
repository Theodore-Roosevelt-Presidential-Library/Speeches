Ladies and Gentlemen:

I am very glad to greet you. I have enjoyed heartily coming through your beautiful state all to-day. I am glad to greet the old folks and glad to greet the little fellows.

It is a great thing what you get from the farm, but what you turn out of the school house is as important, for [-xx-] upon that depends what [-[illegible]-] grade of citizenship we are going to have in the future

This state has been made what it is by the character of the men and women who first came out here on the prairie , who did the hard work,(and there is not any harder [-xxxxxx-] and rougher work than that of the pioneers ,) and put it through good times and evil times until finally[-x-] success came. Success can be kept on much the [+same+] terms upon which it was won. Something can be done by law, something by administration of the law; but nothing can take the place of the man's own courage and hardihood and honesty and sincerity of purpose. [?If?] we have kept those we can make the future as great as the past If we lose them no one can help us, for no one can get out of us what [-[illegible]-] is not in us [-[illegible]-]. I want to see us preserve the economic system, the governmental policies under which we have won these great [-[?prosperity?]-] triumphs; but more than all I want to see the men and women so handle themselves that the average standard of citizenship in the future will be what it has been in the past. If that is kept up we can rest easy in our minds as to what the future will bring forth.

I shall not try to make a long speech to you. I thank you for having come here to see me and I greet you, and am glad to see that the state is looking so [-[illegible]-] [+prosperous,+]
