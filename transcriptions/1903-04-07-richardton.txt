My fellow Citizens:

I am awfully glad to see you my old neighbors and friends. As I have come through North Dakota to-day the farther west I have {g tten|gotten} the more I felt just exactly as if I was getting {ba ck|back} to my home. There is hardly a town we have come to [-that-][+where+] there [-is-][+are+] not some friends {wi th|with} whom I worked in the [-xix-] past days, at[+whose+] {housesI|houses I} slept in the old times. Twenty-three years ago I came to Dakota . It was a territory then. It was twenty years ago that I first worked around here.

I recollect in the old days I used to ship cattle from Dickinson, and half of the time that I would go by here it would be on a cattle train.

I wish you well , and am very glad to have had the chance of greeting you. (Cheers and applause)

[note: "that" and "is" are struck through by hand, with "where" and "are" written above by hand. "whose" after "at" appears to be typed in afterwards below the line, with a hand caret.]
[note: "[-xix-]" before "past days" is x-ed out on the typewriter. In "Dakota" a hand stroke is made over the "k"; in "awfully" there is a stray mark after "u". The folio number appears to be written as 96 over another figure.]
