Mr. Mayor, and you, my fellow Americans:   I thank you from my heart for your greeting and I know you will not grudge my[-x-] saying that of all those present I feel peculiarly pleased to meet here my comrades, the men of your Massachusetts regiment with whom I served at Santiago.   Ladies and gentlemen, the men who went to Cuba were your brothers, sons, kinsfolk, neighbors, and the men who served in the Philippines in the uniform of the American army have been again your brothers, sons, and neighbors.   Last night I spent at Northfield.   Two centur[+i+]es and over ago Northfield was the frontier[+,+] [-[?and?]-] we have Massachusetts now because we were not afraid to expand then[+,+] and we are not afraid to expand now (Applause).   Our destiny [?unexpectedly?] took us to the Philippines . [-xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx-] When you[+ and I,+] my comrades here , went down {toCuba|to Cuba}, I don't suppose any {ofus|of us} thought much about the Philippines. We didn't know much about them - I didn't.   We got there.   We {foun d|found} that we had a job to do {a d|and} we did it, and we did it well (Applause)  What is more, we have no apologies to make for it.   Our soldiers in the Philippines have been attacked because occasionally one of them did something wrong.   There were occasional instances of wrongdoing, and wherever it has been possible to find [-[?them?]-] him out the offender has been punished.   But I ask you when they blame overmuch Uncle Sam's men in blue fighting for their lives against a treacherous foe, in the heart-breaking work of jungle warfare  -- I ask you if our critics remembered in speaking of the occasional shortcomings of the men who did wrong under the stress of such terrible temptation - if they remembered that we are not altogether immaculate at home (Applause).   I would be sorry to have any one of our cities, even the best - even Springfield - judged by the record of its police courts.   If you paid attention purely to that you could make out a pretty bad showing for any city in the land. Occasionally soldiers in the Philippines did wrong.   Wherever possible those men have been {punishedand|punished and} the misdeeds rectified.   But on the whole the men who for three years in the Philippine Islands have followed the [pb 154 424:0634] flag of the United States have added a new page to the honor roll of the nation (Applause).   Never have troops of ours warred under greater difficulties than those in the Philippines.   Troops of ours have warred against more dangerous foes, but never in a climate and under conditions which called for such resolute perseverance on the part of the men.   In the steaming heat of the tropic jungles, starving, footsore, so weary that they dropped asleep in the mud wherever they happened to fall down, at every step fearing ambuscade from a foe who was felt before he was seen, and among a population that greeted with friendliness the oncoming troops and seized the stragglers and put them to death by torture.   Those men had indeed a heavy burden to bear; and I think that the men who sat at home in ease could have afforded to be more lenient in bearing judgment against them (Applause).

And now just one word in closing.   I have really enjoyed the ten days I have spent in New England.   I have greatly enjoyed the time that I have been in Massachusetts.   I do not feel as though there was anything that I could teach, for all that I can do is to give expression to the homely truths in which you and I believe.   I was glad to be in New England on Labor Day.   Labor  Day is rightly an American holiday, for every American who is worth his salt works, and if he doesn't there is something wrong with him (Applause).    Dwight L. Moody, who founded the school at which I was last night, used to say that the devil tempted most men, but that a lazy man tempted the devil.   The law of work is the law of successful American life. Play too.   I believe in having play in which you play and play hard - but work hard too.   Remember that work is the business and play the play of life.

Now another thing:  In speaking to you of Massachusetts here it is hardly necessary to say to an audience like this that there is no patent device for obtaining good citizenship, good government.   Government is us - that is all.   It is our actions shifted through certain channels and given expression to by our representatives.   There is not any patent way to obtain it.   You will obtain it just exactly as it always has been obtained in the past - by decency, honesty, courage, and common sense on the part of the citizens.   Many and grave problems confront us.   We need to face them with

[pb 155 424:0634]

all the intelligence, all the cool sanity, there is at our command.   But let us bear steadily in mind that fundamentally the only way by which this country can be [-kept and made-][+made and kept+] great is by keeping high the standard of average citizenship, by keeping high the standard of manhood and womanhood in the nation. If on the whole we have good men and sensible men as citizens we will have good government and the nation will go forward; and if the average standard of citizenship falls then no law and no constitution will make this government a success.   Each for each and each for all.   Each man must work for himself first, because if he doesn't carry his own weight he is a burden to the community.   Each man must do his duty by himself and by those dependent upon him.   Furthermore, if he is to be a good citizen each man must remember his duty to his neighbor.   There is not one of us here, I don't {came|care} who he is, who does not at times stumble, and shame upon the man who does not then stretch forth the helping hand.   Help your brother who stumbles, but remember if he lies down there is no use in {tryting|trying} to carry him.   If he will not walk, then to carry him is bad for him and bad for you too.   Help him if he stumbles, and help him in the only way by which it is really possible to help any man -- help him to help himself (Great applause).
