My fellow citizens, my fellow Americans, men and women of Vermont:

I have counted myself fortunate to be in Vermont and doubly fortunate to be here on Labor Day.(Cheers and applause) Labor Day is a typical American holiday(Cheers and applause), for the typical American is a[-x-] man who works,(Cries of "That is so!) a man who works for himself, but who also knows how to work in combination with others.(Cheers and applause) And while I am very glad indeed to be greeted by all of you and thank you for the greeting, the rest will excuse my saying that I am particularly glad to be greeted by the representatives of organized labor.(Cheers and applause) This is in many ways a typical community. You have in this community representatives of the country and the city; that is you have the farmer, you have the wage-worker, you have the capitalist, all represented here and in the neighborhood, and gentlemen[+,+] it is in just such a neighborhood as this that we have a right to expect the development of those traits of character which we wish to think of as typically American. In the first place, in a small city, in a city where the town and country come close together it is [+more+] possible for men to realize [-xxxx-] what the government [-ix-] actually is than [?I?]t is possible to bring most men to realize in an enormous mass of people [illegible]uch as you see in the great cities. Here [-ix-] it is brought home more to [illegible]s that the government is us. We are the government;(Cheers and applaus[+)+] [illegible]ou and I, all of us. Now something can be done through government. We [illegible]eed good laws and an honest administration of the law. We need laws which [?sh?]all secure fair play as between man and man, a square chance for a man t[?o?] win on his merits. As the social and economic conditions become more c[?o?]mplex there has to be an increase in the sphere of legislation. But t[?h?]ere is an enormous amount that law cannot do. A very, very great deal c[?a?]n be done by association among ourselves[-,-][+—+] such associations as you see re[?p?]resented here today[+—+] [?a?]ssociations which will teach [?us?] practically[+,+] in the best way that the lesson can be taught[+,+] by applying it[+,+] that not only must each work for himself, but that each must work for all; that we can accomplish by [-[?successful?]-][+united+] self-help much, and that there yet remains an immense amount to be done by individual selfhelp in every association, [-from the [?state, from the nation,?] [illegible] [?union, to a corporation.?]-]

from the state, from the nation, to a union, to a corporation. We find that a man who is to accomplish good for his fellows must steadily endeavor not to be a passenger; he [+must+] pull his own weight and a little more.(Cheers and applause) There isn't any real place in our body politic[-[?s?]-] for the idler. The law of healthy {mational|national} life, and particularly so with us[+,+] is the law of work. If [-the-][+a+] man does his work well and it is honorable work, that is what we have a right to ask. Some work in one way and some in another, but as long as [-the-][+a+] man deals fairly with his neighbor, does not try to wrong him in any way, and as long as he works and it is work worth doing, he is a good citizen, and if he does not fulfill those attributes then he is a bad citizen.

As I say[+,+] each man must be able to work in conjunction with others[+,+] and he must also be able to work by himself. Every man of us at times stumbles; every man of us at times needs a helping hand[-.-][+;+] and shame to the man who when the need comes does not stretch out a helping hand to his brother(Applause). Every man stumbles and should then be helped. But if he lies down [+it+] [-there-] is no use to carry him(Cheers and applause). He has got to try his best to walk, then help him on if you are worth anything; but if he [-ix-] lies down [+it+] [-[?there?]-] is no use.(Applause)

There is no secret rule for good citizenship. I mean there is no specific, no patent for good citizenship. A man has got to have not only one quality, but several qualities, in order to make a good citizen, in order to make it worth our while to try to act with him. In the first {pàce|place} he has got to be honest, decent, fair in his dealings, and not just talk about it,[+—+] to be square in his dealings, and [-[?got?]-] to begin at home. If a man isn't a good husband, a good father, he isn't a good citizen. It is a poor kind of creature [+who is+] always willing to reform [-x-] the world but will not begin at home. He has got to be honest, and that is not enough. An honest man who is afraid is of mighty little good in the world. You have got to have courage with honesty. Honesty and courage[+—+] and those two are not enough. I do not care how honest and how brave a man is, if he is a natural born fool you can do nothing with him. To have the proper standard of citizenship we must have honesty, we must have courage, and we must have in addition the saving grace of common sense.(Applause)
